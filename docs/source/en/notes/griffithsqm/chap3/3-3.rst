Problem 3.3
==================

For arbitrary functions `u,v`, define

.. math::
    D(u,v)\coloneqq
    \braket{u|\hat{Q}v}-\braket{\hat{Q}u|v}.

By assumption, `D(h,h)=0` for every `h`. First, take `h=f+g`. Using the sesquilinearity of the inner product gives

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        0=D(f+g,f+g)
        &=D(f,f)+D(f,g)+D(g,f)+D(g,g)
        \\
        &=D(f,g)+D(g,f).
    \end{aligned}

Therefore,

.. math::
    D(f,g)+D(g,f)=0.

Next, take `h=f+ig`. Noting that the coefficient `i` in the first argument is complex conjugated, we obtain

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        0&=D(f+ig,f+ig)
        \\
        &=D(f,f)+iD(f,g)-iD(g,f)+D(g,g)
        \\
        &=iD(f,g)-iD(g,f),
    \end{aligned}

so

.. math::
    D(f,g)-D(g,f)=0.

Combining the sum and difference equations gives `D(f,g)=D(g,f)=0`. Therefore, for arbitrary `f,g`,

.. math::
    \braket{f|\hat{Q}g}
    =\braket{\hat{Q}f|g}.

Conversely, setting `g=f` in this equation gives `\braket{f|\hat{Q}f}=\braket{\hat{Q}f|f}`. Therefore, the two definitions are equivalent.
