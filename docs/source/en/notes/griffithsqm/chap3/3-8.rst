Problem 3.8
==================

a.  The operator in Example 3.1 is `\hat{Q}=i\,\dd/\dd\phi`, and its normalized eigenfunctions are

    .. math::
        f_n(\phi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-in\phi},
        \quad n=0,\pm1,\pm2,\ldots.

    The eigenvalues are the integers `q_n=n`. Also, for distinct integers `m,n`,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \braket{f_m|f_n}
            &=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
            e^{im\phi}e^{-in\phi}\,\dd\phi
            \\
            &=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
            e^{i(m-n)\phi}\,\dd\phi
            \\
            &= \left.\frac{e^{i(m-n)\phi}}{i(m-n)}\right|_0^{2\pi} =0.
        \end{aligned}

    Therefore, eigenfunctions belonging to different eigenvalues are orthogonal.

b.  The operator in :ref:`notes/griffithsqm/chap3/3-7:Problem 3.7` is `\hat{Q}=\dd^2/\dd\phi^2`, and its normalized eigenfunctions are

    .. math::
        u_n(\phi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{in\phi},
        \quad n\in\Z.

    The eigenvalues are `q_n=-n^2`. Also, for distinct integers `m,n`,

    .. math::
        \braket{u_m|u_n}
        =\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}
        e^{-im\phi}e^{in\phi}\,\dd\phi
        =0,

    so all eigenfunctions with different values of `n` are orthogonal.
