Problem 3.13
==================

From Equation (3.55),

.. math::
    \Psi(x,t)
    =\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}
    \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
    e^{ipx/\hbar}\Phi(p,t)\,\dd p.

For the exponential,

.. math::
    xe^{ipx/\hbar}
    =-i\hbar\pdv{}{p}e^{ipx/\hbar}.

Therefore, multiplying both sides by `x` gives

.. math::
    x\Psi(x,t)
    =\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}
    \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
    \left(
        -i\hbar\pdv{}{p}e^{ipx/\hbar}
    \right)\Phi(p,t)\,\dd p.

Integrating by parts with respect to `p`, we obtain

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        x\Psi(x,t)
        &=-\frac{i\hbar}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}
        \left[e^{ipx/\hbar}\Phi(p,t)\right]_{-\infty}^{\infty}
        \\
        &\quad+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}
        \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
        e^{ipx/\hbar}
        \left(i\hbar\pdv{\Phi}{p}\right)\,\dd p.
    \end{aligned}

Assuming `\Phi(p,t)\to0` as `p\to\pm\infty`, the boundary term vanishes. Since the Fourier transform preserves inner products,

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        \braket{x}
        &=\braket{\Psi|x\Psi}
        \\
        &=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
        \Phi(p,t)^*
        \left(i\hbar\pdv{\Phi(p,t)}{p}\right)\,\dd p
        \\
        &=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
        \Phi^*
        \left(i\hbar\pdv{}{p}\right)
        \Phi\,\dd p.
    \end{aligned}
