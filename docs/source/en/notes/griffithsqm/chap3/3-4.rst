Problem 3.4
==================

a.  If `\hat{Q}` and `\hat{R}` are Hermitian operators, then

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \braket{f|(\hat{Q}+\hat{R})g}
            &=\braket{f|\hat{Q}g}
            +\braket{f|\hat{R}g}
            \\
            &=\braket{\hat{Q}f|g}
            +\braket{\hat{R}f|g}
            \\
            &=\braket{(\hat{Q}+\hat{R})f|g}.
        \end{aligned}

    Therefore, `\hat{Q}+\hat{R}` is also Hermitian.

b.  If `\hat{Q}` is Hermitian, then

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \braket{f|\alpha\hat{Q}g}
            &=\alpha\braket{f|\hat{Q}g}
            \\
            &=\alpha\braket{\hat{Q}f|g}
            \\
            &=\braket{\alpha^*\hat{Q}f|g}.
        \end{aligned}

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        (\alpha\hat{Q})^\dagger=\alpha^*\hat{Q}.

    If `\hat{Q}\neq0`, the necessary and sufficient condition for `\alpha\hat{Q}` to be Hermitian is

    .. math::
        \alpha^*=\alpha\quad\iff\quad
        \alpha\in\R.

    If `\hat{Q}=0`, however, `\alpha\hat{Q}` is the zero operator for every `\alpha` and is therefore trivially Hermitian.

c.  For two Hermitian operators,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \braket{f|\hat{Q}\hat{R}g}
            &=\braket{\hat{Q}f|\hat{R}g}
            \\
            &=\braket{\hat{R}\hat{Q}f|g},
        \end{aligned}

    and hence

    .. math::
        (\hat{Q}\hat{R})^\dagger
        =\hat{R}\hat{Q}.

    Therefore, the necessary and sufficient condition for the product `\hat{Q}\hat{R}` to be Hermitian is

    .. math::
        \hat{Q}\hat{R}=\hat{R}\hat{Q}
        \quad\iff\quad
        [\hat{Q},\hat{R}]=0.

    Thus, the product is Hermitian if and only if the two operators commute.

d.  For the position operator, `x` is real, and therefore

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \braket{f|\hat{x}g}
            &=\int f^*(x)xg(x)\,\dd x
            \\
            &=\int\left(xf(x)\right)^*g(x)\,\dd x
            \\
            &=\braket{\hat{x}f|g}.
        \end{aligned}

    Thus, `\hat{x}` is Hermitian.

    Define the kinetic-energy part of the Hamiltonian by

    .. math::
        \hat{T}\coloneqq-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\dv{^2}{x^2}.

    Integrating by parts twice gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \braket{f|\hat{T}g}
            &=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\int f^*g''\,\dd x
            \\
            &=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}
            \left[f^*g'-f'^*g\right]_{\text{boundary}}
            -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\int f''^*g\,\dd x
            \\
            &=\braket{\hat{T}f|g},
        \end{aligned}

    where the boundary term has been assumed to vanish. Also, since a physical potential `V(x)` is real-valued,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \braket{f|Vg}
            &=\int f^*Vg\,\dd x
            \\
            &=\int(Vf)^*g\,\dd x
            \\
            &=\braket{Vf|g}.
        \end{aligned}

    Thus, the potential operator is also Hermitian. Therefore, by \(a), their sum

    .. math::
        \hat{H}
        =-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\dv{^2}{x^2}+V(x)

    is also Hermitian.
