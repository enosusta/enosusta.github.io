Problem 3.25
==================

By orthonormality,

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        \hat{H}|1\rangle
        &=\epsilon\left(|1\rangle+|2\rangle\right)
        \\
        \hat{H}|2\rangle
        &=\epsilon\left(|1\rangle-|2\rangle\right).
    \end{aligned}

Therefore, the matrix of the Hamiltonian in the basis `\{|1\rangle,|2\rangle\}` is

.. math::
    \bm{H}
    =\epsilon
    \begin{pmatrix}
        1 & 1
        \\
        1 & -1
    \end{pmatrix}.

Writing the eigenvalue as `E=\epsilon\lambda`, the characteristic equation is

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        0
        &=\det
        \begin{pmatrix}
            1-\lambda & 1
            \\
            1 & -1-\lambda
        \end{pmatrix}
        \\
        &=\lambda^2-2,
    \end{aligned}

so the eigenvalues are

.. math::
    E_\pm=\pm\sqrt{2}\,\epsilon.

Now let the eigenvector be `|E\rangle=a|1\rangle+b|2\rangle`. Then

.. math::
    \left(1-\lambda\right)a+b=0 \implies
    b=\left(\lambda-1\right)a.

For `\lambda=\sqrt{2}`, we have `b/a=\sqrt{2}-1=\tan(\pi/8)`, so we may choose the normalized eigenvector

.. math::
    |E_+\rangle
    =\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{8}\right)|1\rangle
    +\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{8}\right)|2\rangle.

The other normalized eigenvector, which is orthogonal to this one, is

.. math::
    |E_-\rangle
    =-\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{8}\right)|1\rangle
    +\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{8}\right)|2\rangle.
