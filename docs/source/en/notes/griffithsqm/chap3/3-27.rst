Problem 3.27
==================

a.  Any vector `|\alpha\rangle` can be expanded as

    .. math::
        |\alpha\rangle
        =\sum_n a_n|e_n\rangle,
        \quad
        a_n=\braket{e_n|\alpha}.

    Using the eigenvalue equation gives

    .. math::
        \hat{Q}|\alpha\rangle
        =\sum_n a_n\hat{Q}|e_n\rangle
        =\sum_n q_na_n|e_n\rangle.

    On the other hand,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \left( \sum_n q_n\ket{e_n}\bra{e_n} \right) |\alpha\rangle
            &=\sum_n q_n|e_n\rangle
            \braket{e_n|\alpha}
            \\
            &=\sum_n q_na_n|e_n\rangle.
        \end{aligned}

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        \hat{Q}
        =\sum_n q_n|e_n\rangle\langle e_n|.

b.  Define the projection operators by

    .. math::
        \hat{P}_n\coloneqq|e_n\rangle\langle e_n|.

    Orthonormality and completeness give

    .. math::
        \hat{P}_n\hat{P}_m
        =\delta_{nm}\hat{P}_n,
        \quad
        \sum_n\hat{P}_n=\mathbf{1}.

    Therefore, repeated multiplication of the result in \(a) gives

    .. math::
        \hat{Q}^{\,k}
        =\left(\sum_n q_n\hat{P}_n\right)^k
        =\sum_n q_n^k\hat{P}_n.

    Substituting this expression into the power series for the exponential gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            e^{\hat{Q}}
            &=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
            \frac{\hat{Q}^{\,k}}{k!}
            \\
            &=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}
            \sum_n q_n^k\hat{P}_n
            \\
            &=\sum_n
            \left(
                \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{q_n^k}{k!}
            \right)\hat{P}_n
            \\
            &=\sum_n e^{q_n}|e_n\rangle\langle e_n|.
        \end{aligned}
