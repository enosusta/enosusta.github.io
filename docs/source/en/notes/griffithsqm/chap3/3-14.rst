Problem 3.14
==================

a.  From the definition of the commutator,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            [\hat{A}+\hat{B},\hat{C}]
            &=\left(\hat{A}+\hat{B}\right)\hat{C}
            -\hat{C}\left(\hat{A}+\hat{B}\right)
            \\
            &=\hat{A}\hat{C}-\hat{C}\hat{A}
            +\hat{B}\hat{C}-\hat{C}\hat{B}
            \\
            &=[\hat{A},\hat{C}]+[\hat{B},\hat{C}].
        \end{aligned}

    Also,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            [\hat{A}\hat{B},\hat{C}]
            &=\hat{A}\hat{B}\hat{C}-\hat{C}\hat{A}\hat{B}
            \\
            &=\hat{A}\hat{B}\hat{C}
            -\hat{A}\hat{C}\hat{B}
            +\hat{A}\hat{C}\hat{B}
            -\hat{C}\hat{A}\hat{B}
            \\
            &=\hat{A}\left(\hat{B}\hat{C}-\hat{C}\hat{B}\right)
            +\left(\hat{A}\hat{C}-\hat{C}\hat{A}\right)\hat{B}
            \\
            &=\hat{A}[\hat{B},\hat{C}]
            +[\hat{A},\hat{C}]\hat{B}.
        \end{aligned}

b.  We use mathematical induction. For `n=1`,

    .. math::
        [x,\hat{p}]=i\hbar.

    Suppose that

    .. math::
        [x^n,\hat{p}]=i\hbar nx^{n-1}

    holds for `n`. Setting `\hat{A}=x^n`, `\hat{B}=x`, and `\hat{C}=\hat{p}` in \(a) gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            [x^{n+1},\hat{p}]
            &=x^n[x,\hat{p}]+[x^n,\hat{p}]x
            \\
            &=x^n(i\hbar)+(i\hbar nx^{n-1})x
            \\
            &=i\hbar(n+1)x^n,
        \end{aligned}

    so the result also holds for `n+1`. Therefore, for every positive integer `n`,

    .. math::
        [x^n,\hat{p}]=i\hbar nx^{n-1}.

c.  Suppose that `f(x)` has the Taylor expansion

    .. math::
        f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_nx^n.

    By \(b),

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            [f(x),\hat{p}]
            &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_n[x^n,\hat{p}]
            \\
            &=i\hbar\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nc_nx^{n-1}
            \\
            &=i\hbar\dv{f}{x}.
        \end{aligned}

d.  From Equation (2.54), the commutation relation `[\hat{a}_-,\hat{a}_+]=1`, and \(a),

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            [\hat{H},\hat{a}_+]
            &=\hbar\omega
            [\hat{a}_-\hat{a}_+,\hat{a}_+]
            \\
            &=\hbar\omega\left(
                \hat{a}_-[\hat{a}_+,\hat{a}_+]
                +[\hat{a}_-,\hat{a}_+]\hat{a}_+
            \right)
            \\
            &=\hbar\omega\hat{a}_+.
        \end{aligned}

    Similarly, using `[\hat{a}_+,\hat{a}_-]=-1`,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            [\hat{H},\hat{a}_-]
            &=\hbar\omega
            [\hat{a}_-\hat{a}_+,\hat{a}_-]
            \\
            &=\hbar\omega\left(
                \hat{a}_-[\hat{a}_+,\hat{a}_-]
                +[\hat{a}_-,\hat{a}_-]\hat{a}_+
            \right)
            \\
            &=-\hbar\omega\hat{a}_-.
        \end{aligned}
