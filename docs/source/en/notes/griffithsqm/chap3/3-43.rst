Problem 3.43
==================

a.  For a normalized state `|\psi\rangle`, define

    .. math::
        |f\rangle
        =\left(\hat{A}-\braket{A}\right)|\psi\rangle,
        \quad
        |g\rangle
        =\left(\hat{B}-\braket{B}\right)|\psi\rangle.

    Then

    .. math::
        \braket{f|f}=\sigma_A^2,
        \quad
        \braket{g|g}=\sigma_B^2.

    By the Schwarz inequality,

    .. math::
        \sigma_A^2\sigma_B^2
        =\braket{f|f}\braket{g|g}
        \geq|\braket{f|g}|^2.

    Let `z\coloneqq\braket{f|g}`. Since `\hat{A}` and `\hat{B}` are Hermitian,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            z
            &=\left\langle
                \left(\hat{A}-\braket{A}\right)
                \left(\hat{B}-\braket{B}\right)
            \right\rangle
            \\
            z^*
            &=\left\langle
                \left(\hat{B}-\braket{B}\right)
                \left(\hat{A}-\braket{A}\right)
            \right\rangle.
        \end{aligned}

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            2\operatorname{Re}z
            &=z+z^*
            \\
            &=\braket{AB+BA}
            -2\braket{A}\braket{B}
            =\braket{D}
            \\
            2i\operatorname{Im}z
            &=z-z^*
            \\
            &=\braket{[A,B]}
            =i\braket{C}.
        \end{aligned}

    Hence,

    .. math::
        \operatorname{Re}z=\frac{1}{2}\braket{D},
        \quad
        \operatorname{Im}z=\frac{1}{2}\braket{C},

    and substituting back into the Schwarz inequality gives

    .. math::
        \sigma_A^2\sigma_B^2
        \geq\frac{1}{4}
        \left(\braket{C}^2+\braket{D}^2\right).

b.  If `B=A`, then `[A,A]=0`, so `\hat{C}=0`. Also,

    .. math::
        \hat{D}=2\hat{A}^2-2\braket{A}^2,

    and its expectation value is

    .. math::
        \braket{D}
        =2\left(\braket{A^2}-\braket{A}^2\right)
        =2\sigma_A^2.

    Therefore, the strengthened uncertainty relation becomes

    .. math::
        \sigma_A^4
        \geq\frac{1}{4}\left(2\sigma_A^2\right)^2
        =\sigma_A^4,

    so equality always holds.
