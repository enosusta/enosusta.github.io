Problem 3.7
==================

a.  By assumption,

    .. math::
        \hat{Q}f=qf,\quad
        \hat{Q}g=qg.

    For arbitrary complex numbers `\alpha,\beta`,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \hat{Q}(\alpha f+\beta g)
            &=\alpha\hat{Q}f+\beta\hat{Q}g
            \\
            &=\alpha qf+\beta qg
            \\
            &=q(\alpha f+\beta g).
        \end{aligned}

b.  Differentiating twice gives

    .. math::
        \dv{^2}{x^2}e^x=e^x,\quad
        \dv{^2}{x^2}e^{-x}=e^{-x},

    so `f=e^x` and `g=e^{-x}` both belong to the eigenvalue `1`. By \(a),

    .. math::
        u(x)=\frac{f+g}{2}=\cosh x,\quad
        v(x)=\frac{f-g}{2}=\sinh x

    are also eigenfunctions with eigenvalue `1`. Since `u` is even and `v` is odd, the product `u^*v=\cosh x\sinh x` is odd, and

    .. math::
        \braket{u|v}
        =\int_{-1}^{1}\cosh x\sinh x\,\dd x
        =0.

    Therefore, these two functions are orthogonal on the interval `(-1,1)`.
