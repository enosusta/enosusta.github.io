Problem 3.29
==================

a.  We use mathematical induction. For `n=1`,

    .. math::
        [\hat{A},\hat{B}]=\hat{C},

    so the claim holds. Suppose that, for some positive integer `n`,

    .. math::
        [\hat{A}^{\,n},\hat{B}]
        =n\hat{A}^{\,n-1}\hat{C}.

    Using the product rule for commutators gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            [\hat{A}^{\,n+1},\hat{B}]
            &=[\hat{A}^{\,n}\hat{A},\hat{B}]
            \\
            &=\hat{A}^{\,n}[\hat{A},\hat{B}]
            +[\hat{A}^{\,n},\hat{B}]\hat{A}
            \\
            &=\hat{A}^{\,n}\hat{C}
            +n\hat{A}^{\,n-1}\hat{C}\hat{A}
            \\
            &=\left(n+1\right)\hat{A}^{\,n}\hat{C}.
        \end{aligned}

    In the last equality, we used the fact that `\hat{A}` and `\hat{C}` commute. Therefore,

    .. math::
        [\hat{A}^{\,n},\hat{B}]
        =n\hat{A}^{\,n-1}\hat{C}

    holds for every positive integer `n`.

b.  Expanding the exponential operator as a power series and using the result from \(a), we obtain

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            [e^{\lambda\hat{A}},\hat{B}]
            &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
            \frac{\lambda^n}{n!}
            [\hat{A}^{\,n},\hat{B}]
            \\
            &=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
            \frac{\lambda^n}{n!}
            n\hat{A}^{\,n-1}\hat{C}
            \\
            &=\lambda\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}
            \frac{\lambda^r\hat{A}^{\,r}}{r!}\hat{C}
            \\
            &=\lambda e^{\lambda\hat{A}}\hat{C}.
        \end{aligned}

c.  Define the following two operator-valued functions:

    .. math::
        \hat{f}(\lambda)
        =e^{\lambda\left(\hat{A}+\hat{B}\right)},
        \quad
        \hat{g}(\lambda)
        =e^{\lambda\hat{A}}
        e^{\lambda\hat{B}}
        e^{-\lambda^2\hat{C}/2}.

    At `\lambda=0`,

    .. math::
        \hat{f}(0)=\hat{g}(0)=\mathbf{1},

    and `\hat{f}` satisfies

    .. math::
        \dv{\hat{f}}{\lambda}
        =\left(\hat{A}+\hat{B}\right)\hat{f}.

    Next, differentiating `\hat{g}` while preserving the order of the factors, and using the fact that `\hat{C}` commutes with `\hat{A}` and `\hat{B}`, gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \dv{\hat{g}}{\lambda}
            &=\hat{A}e^{\lambda\hat{A}}
            e^{\lambda\hat{B}}e^{-\lambda^2\hat{C}/2}
            \\
            &\quad
            +e^{\lambda\hat{A}}\hat{B}
            e^{\lambda\hat{B}}e^{-\lambda^2\hat{C}/2}
            \\
            &\quad
            -\lambda e^{\lambda\hat{A}}
            e^{\lambda\hat{B}}\hat{C}
            e^{-\lambda^2\hat{C}/2}.
        \end{aligned}

    By \(b),

    .. math::
        e^{\lambda\hat{A}}\hat{B}
        =\hat{B}e^{\lambda\hat{A}}
        +\lambda e^{\lambda\hat{A}}\hat{C},

    so the second term in the derivative is

    .. math::
        e^{\lambda\hat{A}}\hat{B}
        e^{\lambda\hat{B}}e^{-\lambda^2\hat{C}/2}
        =\hat{B}\hat{g}+\lambda\hat{C}\hat{g}.

    The second term on the right-hand side cancels the third term in the derivative, `-\lambda\hat{C}\hat{g}`, and hence

    .. math::
        \dv{\hat{g}}{\lambda}
        =\hat{A}\hat{g}+\hat{B}\hat{g}
        =\left(\hat{A}+\hat{B}\right)\hat{g}.

    The functions `\hat{f}` and `\hat{g}` satisfy the same first-order differential equation with the same initial condition, so `\hat{f}(\lambda)=\hat{g}(\lambda)` for every `\lambda`. In particular, setting `\lambda=1` gives the Baker--Campbell--Hausdorff formula

    .. math::
        e^{\hat{A}+\hat{B}}
        =e^{\hat{A}}e^{\hat{B}}e^{-\hat{C}/2}.
