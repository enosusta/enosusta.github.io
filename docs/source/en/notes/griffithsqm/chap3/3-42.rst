Problem 3.42
==================

a.  Let `a_-|\alpha\rangle=\alpha|\alpha\rangle`, and suppose that the state is normalized. Since `a_+=a_-^\dagger`, taking the Hermitian conjugate gives

    .. math::
        \langle\alpha|a_+
        =\alpha^*\langle\alpha|.

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        \braket{a_-}=\alpha,
        \quad
        \braket{a_+}=\alpha^*,
        \quad
        \braket{a_+a_-}=|\alpha|^2.

    Furthermore, `[a_-,a_+]=1` gives

    .. math::
        \braket{a_-a_+}=|\alpha|^2+1,
        \quad
        \braket{a_-^2}=\alpha^2,
        \quad
        \braket{a_+^2}=\left(\alpha^*\right)^2.

    Define

    .. math::
        x=c\left(a_++a_-\right),
        \quad
        p=id\left(a_+-a_-\right),
        \quad
        c=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}},
        \quad
        d=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar m\omega}{2}}.

    Then

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \braket{x}
            &=c\left(\alpha+\alpha^*\right)
            =\sqrt{\frac{2\hbar}{m\omega}}
            \operatorname{Re}\alpha
            \\
            \braket{p}
            &=id\left(\alpha^*-\alpha\right)
            =\sqrt{2\hbar m\omega}
            \operatorname{Im}\alpha.
        \end{aligned}

    Also,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \braket{x^2}
            &=c^2\left[
                \left(\alpha^*\right)^2
                +|\alpha|^2
                +\left(|\alpha|^2+1\right)
                +\alpha^2
            \right]
            \\
            &=c^2\left[
                \left(\alpha+\alpha^*\right)^2+1
            \right]
            \\
            &=\braket{x}^2+\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega},
        \end{aligned}

    and

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \braket{p^2}
            &=-d^2\left[
                \left(\alpha^*\right)^2
                -|\alpha|^2
                -\left(|\alpha|^2+1\right)
                +\alpha^2
            \right]
            \\
            &=d^2\left[
                1-\left(\alpha-\alpha^*\right)^2
            \right]
            \\
            &=\braket{p}^2+\frac{\hbar m\omega}{2}.
        \end{aligned}

b.  From the results in \(a),

    .. math::
        \sigma_x^2
        =\braket{x^2}-\braket{x}^2
        =\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega},
        \quad
        \sigma_p^2
        =\braket{p^2}-\braket{p}^2
        =\frac{\hbar m\omega}{2},

    and hence

    .. math::
        \sigma_x=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}},
        \quad
        \sigma_p=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar m\omega}{2}},
        \quad
        \sigma_x\sigma_p=\frac{\hbar}{2}.

c.  Apply `a_-` to the expansion in energy eigenstates,

    .. math::
        |\alpha\rangle
        =\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_n|n\rangle.

    This gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            a_-|\alpha\rangle
            &=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
            c_n\sqrt{n}|n-1\rangle
            \\
            &=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}
            c_{r+1}\sqrt{r+1}|r\rangle.
        \end{aligned}

    Since this must equal

    .. math::
        \alpha|\alpha\rangle
        =\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\alpha c_r|r\rangle,

    for each `r` we have

    .. math::
        c_{r+1}\sqrt{r+1}=\alpha c_r,
        \quad
        c_{r+1}=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{r+1}}c_r.

    Repeatedly applying this recurrence relation gives

    .. math::
        c_n=\frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}c_0.

d.  The normalization condition is

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            1=\braket{\alpha|\alpha}
            &=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|c_n|^2
            \\
            &=|c_0|^2
            \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
            \frac{|\alpha|^{2n}}{n!}
            =|c_0|^2e^{|\alpha|^2},
        \end{aligned}

    so we may take

    .. math::
        c_0=e^{-|\alpha|^2/2},
        \quad
        |\alpha\rangle
        =e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}
        \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
        \frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}|n\rangle.

e.  Substituting `E_n=(n+1/2)\hbar\omega`, the state at time `t` is

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            |\alpha;t\rangle
            &=e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}
            \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
            \frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}
            e^{-i\left(n+1/2\right)\omega t}|n\rangle
            \\
            &=e^{-i\omega t/2}e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}
            \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
            \frac{\left(\alpha e^{-i\omega t}\right)^n}
            {\sqrt{n!}}|n\rangle
            \\
            &=e^{-i\omega t/2}
            |\alpha e^{-i\omega t}\rangle.
        \end{aligned}

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        a_-|\alpha;t\rangle
        =\alpha e^{-i\omega t}|\alpha;t\rangle,
        \quad
        \alpha(t)=\alpha e^{-i\omega t}.

f.  Write the complex number `\alpha` as

    .. math::
        \alpha
        =C\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}e^{i\phi}.

    Then

    .. math::
        \alpha(t)
        =C\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}
        e^{i\left(\phi-\omega t\right)}.

    Substituting this into the result of \(a) gives

    .. math::
        \braket{x}(t)
        =\sqrt{\frac{2\hbar}{m\omega}}
        \operatorname{Re}\alpha(t)
        =C\cos\left(\omega t-\phi\right).

    On the other hand, from \(b),

    .. math::
        \sigma_x(t)
        =\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}},

    so the width of the wave packet is independent of time.

g.  Since the ground state satisfies

    .. math::
        a_-|0\rangle=0,

    `|0\rangle` is the coherent state with eigenvalue `\alpha=0`.
