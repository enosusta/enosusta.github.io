Problem 3.21
==================

The Fourier transform of the wave function in :ref:`notes/griffithsqm/chap2/2-42:Problem 2.42` is

.. math::
    \phi(k) = \frac{A}{\sqrt{2a}}\exp\left[-\frac{(k-l)^2}{4a}\right],

which corresponds to a Gaussian distribution with mean `l` and variance `a`. Therefore, we can write the mean and variance of the momentum as

.. math::
    \mu\coloneqq\braket{p}=\hbar l,
    \quad
    s^2\coloneqq\sigma_p^2=a\hbar^2.

The second and fourth moments of a Gaussian distribution are

.. math::
    \braket{p^2}=\mu^2+s^2,
    \quad
    \braket{p^4}=\mu^4+6\mu^2s^2+3s^4,

so

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        \braket{p^2}
        &=\hbar^2\left(l^2+a\right)
        \\
        \braket{p^4}
        &=\hbar^4\left(l^4+6al^2+3a^2\right).
    \end{aligned}

For a free particle, `\hat{H}=\hat{p}^{\,2}/(2m)`, and hence

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        \braket{H}
        &=\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\left(l^2+a\right)
        \\
        \braket{H^2}
        &=\frac{\hbar^4}{4m^2}
        \left(l^4+6al^2+3a^2\right).
    \end{aligned}

It follows that

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        \sigma_H^2
        &=\braket{H^2}-\braket{H}^2
        \\
        &=\frac{\hbar^4}{4m^2}
        \left[
            l^4+6al^2+3a^2
            -\left(l^2+a\right)^2
        \right]
        \\
        &=\frac{\hbar^4}{m^2}
        a\left(l^2+\frac{a}{2}\right),
    \end{aligned}

and therefore

.. math::
    \sigma_H
    =\frac{\hbar^2}{m}
    \sqrt{a\left(l^2+\frac{a}{2}\right)}.

From the result of :ref:`notes/griffithsqm/chap2/2-42:Problem 2.42`,

.. math::
    \sigma_x
    =\frac{1}{2\sqrt{a}}
    \sqrt{1+\left(\frac{2\hbar at}{m}\right)^2},
    \quad
    \dv{\braket{x}}{t}=\frac{\hbar l}{m}.

The two sides of the energy-time uncertainty principle are thus

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        \sigma_H\sigma_x
        &=\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}
        \sqrt{
            \left(l^2+\frac{a}{2}\right)
            \left(1+\left(\frac{2\hbar at}{m}\right)^2\right)
        }
        \\
        \frac{\hbar}{2}
        \left|\dv{\braket{x}}{t}\right|
        &=\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}|l|.
    \end{aligned}

Since

.. math::
    \left(l^2+\frac{a}{2}\right)
    \left(1+\left(\frac{2\hbar at}{m}\right)^2\right)-l^2
    =\frac{a}{2}
    +\left(\frac{2\hbar at}{m}\right)^2\left(l^2+\frac{a}{2}\right)>0,

the energy-time uncertainty principle holds at every time.
