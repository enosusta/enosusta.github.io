Problem 3.40
==================

Expanding the expectation value of position in the energy eigenbasis gives

.. math::
    \braket{x}
    =\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}
    c_m^*c_n
    e^{i\left(E_m-E_n\right)t/\hbar}
    \braket{m|x|n}.

From :ref:`notes/griffithsqm/chap3/3-39:Problem 3.39`, `\braket{m|x|n}` is nonzero only when `m=n\pm1`. Also,

.. math::
    E_{n+1}-E_n=\hbar\omega.

Define

.. math::
    S=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
    \sqrt{n+1}\,c_{n+1}^*c_n.

The sum of the terms with `m=n+1` is

.. math::
    \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}Se^{i\omega t},

and the sum of the terms with `m=n-1` is its complex conjugate. Therefore,

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        \braket{x}
        &=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}
        \left(
            Se^{i\omega t}+S^*e^{-i\omega t}
        \right)
        \\
        &=2\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}
        \operatorname{Re}\left(Se^{i\omega t}\right).
    \end{aligned}

Now define the real constants `C,\phi` by

.. math::
    Ce^{-i\phi}
    =\sqrt{\frac{2\hbar}{m\omega}}S
    =\sqrt{\frac{2\hbar}{m\omega}}
    \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}
    \sqrt{n+1}\,c_{n+1}^*c_n.

Then

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        \braket{x}
        &=\operatorname{Re}\left(
            Ce^{-i\phi}e^{i\omega t}
        \right)
        \\
        &=C\cos\left(\omega t-\phi\right).
    \end{aligned}

For the wave function in :ref:`notes/griffithsqm/chap2/2-40:Problem 2.40`, the nonzero expansion coefficients are

.. math::
    c_0=\frac{3}{5},
    \quad
    c_1=-\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{5},
    \quad
    c_2=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{5},

and all of them are real. Therefore,

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        S
        &=c_1c_0+\sqrt{2}\,c_2c_1
        \\
        &=\left(-\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{5}\right)
        \frac{3}{5}
        +\sqrt{2}
        \left(\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{5}\right)
        \left(-\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{5}\right)
        \\
        &=-\frac{6\sqrt{2}}{25}
        -\frac{8\sqrt{2}}{25}
        =-\frac{14\sqrt{2}}{25},
    \end{aligned}

and hence

.. math::
    Ce^{-i\phi}
    =-\frac{28}{25}
    \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}}.

Therefore,

.. math::
    C=\frac{28}{25}
    \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}},
    \quad
    \phi=\pi\pmod{2\pi}.
