Problem 3.15
==================

Let the Hamiltonian be

.. math::
    \hat{H}=\frac{\hat{p}^{\,2}}{2m}+V(x).

Since `x` and `V(x)` are both functions of position, `[x,V(x)]=0`. Also,

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        [x,\hat{p}^{\,2}]
        &=[x,\hat{p}]\hat{p}
        +\hat{p}[x,\hat{p}]
        \\
        &=(i\hbar)\hat{p}+\hat{p}(i\hbar)
        \\
        &=2i\hbar\hat{p},
    \end{aligned}

and hence

.. math::
    [x,\hat{H}]
    =\frac{1}{2m}[x,\hat{p}^{\,2}]+[x,V]
    =\frac{i\hbar}{m}\hat{p}.

Setting `\hat{A}=x` and `\hat{B}=\hat{H}` in the generalized uncertainty principle (3.62), we obtain

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        \sigma_x^2\sigma_H^2
        &\geq
        \left(
            \frac{1}{2i}\braket{[x,\hat{H}]}
        \right)^2
        \\
        &=\left(
            \frac{1}{2i}\frac{i\hbar}{m}\braket{p}
        \right)^2
        \\
        &=\left(
            \frac{\hbar}{2m}\braket{p}
        \right)^2.
    \end{aligned}

Therefore,

.. math::
    \sigma_x\sigma_H
    \geq\frac{\hbar}{2m}\left|\braket{p}\right|.

Since a stationary state is an eigenstate of the Hamiltonian, its energy variance is

.. math::
    \sigma_H^2
    =\braket{H^2}-\braket{H}^2
    =E^2-E^2
    =0.

Also, `\braket{x}` is independent of time in a stationary state, so Ehrenfest's theorem,

.. math::
    \dv{\braket{x}}{t}
    =\frac{\braket{p}}{m},

implies `\braket{p}=0`. Therefore, the inequality reduces to `0\geq0` and imposes no restriction on the uncertainty in position.
