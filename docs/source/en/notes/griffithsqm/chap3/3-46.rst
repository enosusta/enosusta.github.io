Problem 3.46
==================

a.  Write the standard basis as

    .. math::
        e_1=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\end{pmatrix},
        \quad
        e_2=\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\0\end{pmatrix},
        \quad
        e_3=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}.

    Since `\bm{H}` is already diagonal, its eigenvalues and eigenspaces are

    .. math::
        \hbar\omega
        \longleftrightarrow e_1,
        \quad
        2\hbar\omega
        \longleftrightarrow
        \operatorname{span}\{e_2,e_3\}.

    The eigenvalues and normalized eigenvectors of `\bm{A}` are

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \lambda
            &\longleftrightarrow
            \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
            \begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\0\end{pmatrix}
            \\
            -\lambda
            &\longleftrightarrow
            \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
            \begin{pmatrix}1\\-1\\0\end{pmatrix}
            \\
            2\lambda
            &\longleftrightarrow e_3.
        \end{aligned}

    Similarly, for `\bm{B}`,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            2\mu
            &\longleftrightarrow e_1
            \\
            \mu
            &\longleftrightarrow
            \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
            \begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\1\end{pmatrix}
            \\
            -\mu
            &\longleftrightarrow
            \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
            \begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\-1\end{pmatrix}.
        \end{aligned}

b.  Direct matrix multiplication gives the expectation values at `t=0`:

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \braket{H}_0
            &=\hbar\omega\left(
                |c_1|^2+2|c_2|^2+2|c_3|^2
            \right)
            \\
            &=\hbar\omega\left(2-|c_1|^2\right)
            \\
            \braket{A}_0
            &=\lambda\left(
                c_1^*c_2+c_2^*c_1+2|c_3|^2
            \right)
            \\
            &=2\lambda\left[
                \operatorname{Re}\left(c_1^*c_2\right)
                +|c_3|^2
            \right]
            \\
            \braket{B}_0
            &=\mu\left(
                2|c_1|^2+c_2^*c_3+c_3^*c_2
            \right)
            \\
            &=2\mu\left[
                |c_1|^2
                +\operatorname{Re}\left(c_2^*c_3\right)
            \right].
        \end{aligned}

c.  Time evolution attaches a phase to each energy eigencomponent of the Hamiltonian, so

    .. math::
        |S(t)\rangle
        =\begin{pmatrix}
            c_1e^{-i\omega t}
            \\
            c_2e^{-2i\omega t}
            \\
            c_3e^{-2i\omega t}
        \end{pmatrix}.

    The possible results of an energy measurement and their probabilities are

    .. math::
        P_H(\hbar\omega)=|c_1|^2,
        \quad
        P_H(2\hbar\omega)=|c_2|^2+|c_3|^2.

    For a measurement of `A`, the inner products with its eigenvectors give

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            P_A(\lambda;t)
            &=\frac{1}{2}
            \left|
                c_1e^{-i\omega t}+c_2e^{-2i\omega t}
            \right|^2
            \\
            P_A(-\lambda;t)
            &=\frac{1}{2}
            \left|
                c_1e^{-i\omega t}-c_2e^{-2i\omega t}
            \right|^2
            \\
            P_A(2\lambda;t)
            &=|c_3|^2.
        \end{aligned}

    Finally, the possible results of a measurement of `B` and their probabilities are

    .. math::
        P_B(2\mu;t)=|c_1|^2,
        \quad
        P_B(\mu;t)=\frac{1}{2}|c_2+c_3|^2,
        \quad
        P_B(-\mu;t)=\frac{1}{2}|c_2-c_3|^2.
