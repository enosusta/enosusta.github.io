Problem 3.48
==================

a.  First, let

    .. math::
        D(\hat{p})
        =\left\{
            f\mathrel{\big|}
            f,f'\in L^2(0,a),\quad f(0)=f(a)=0
        \right\}.

    For `f\in D(\hat{p})`, integration by parts gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \braket{g|\hat{p}f}
            &=\int_0^a g(x)^*\left(-i\hbar f'(x)\right)\,\dd x
            \\
            &=-i\hbar\left[g^*f\right]_0^a
            +\int_0^a i\hbar g'(x)^*f(x)\,\dd x
            \\
            &=\int_0^a\left(-i\hbar g'(x)\right)^*
            f(x)\,\dd x
            \\
            &=\braket{\hat{p}^\dagger g|f}.
        \end{aligned}

    The boundary term vanishes solely because `f(0)=f(a)=0`, so no condition need be imposed on `g(0)` or `g(a)`. Thus, the action of the adjoint is

    .. math::
        \hat{p}^\dagger g=-i\hbar g',

    which is the same as that of the original operator, but its domain is

    .. math::
        D(\hat{p}^\dagger)
        =\left\{
            g\mathrel{\big|}g,g'\in L^2(0,a)
        \right\}.

    Thus, the operator is Hermitian because its action agrees with that of its adjoint on their common domain, but

    .. math::
        D(\hat{p})
        \subsetneq D(\hat{p}^\dagger),

    so it is not self-adjoint.

b.  Now let

    .. math::
        D_\lambda(\hat{p})
        =\left\{
            f\mathrel{\big|}
            f,f'\in L^2(0,a),\quad f(a)=\lambda f(0)
        \right\}.

    The boundary term produced by integration by parts is

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            -i\hbar\left[g^*f\right]_0^a
            &=-i\hbar\left[
                g^*(a)f(a)-g^*(0)f(0)
            \right]
            \\
            &=-i\hbar\left[
                \lambda g^*(a)-g^*(0)
            \right]f(0).
        \end{aligned}

    The condition for this term to vanish for every `f\in D_\lambda(\hat{p})` is

    .. math::
        g^*(0)=\lambda g^*(a)
        \iff
        g(0)=\lambda^*g(a).

    If `\lambda\neq0`, this can also be written as

    .. math::
        g(a)=\frac{1}{\lambda^*}g(0).

    Therefore, the necessary and sufficient condition for the original boundary condition and the adjoint boundary condition to coincide is

    .. math::
        \lambda=\frac{1}{\lambda^*}
        \iff
        |\lambda|=1.

c.  On the half-line, the boundary term at infinity vanishes, so the boundary form is simply

    .. math::
        -i\hbar\left[g^*f\right]_0^\infty
        =i\hbar g^*(0)f(0).

    For this to vanish for all `f,g` in the same domain, one must impose `f(0)=0`. However, the adjoint determined by this domain places no restriction on `g(0)`, so the two domains do not coincide.
