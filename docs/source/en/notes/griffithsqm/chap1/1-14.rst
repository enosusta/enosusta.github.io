Problem 1.14
======================

a.  The time-dependent Schrödinger equation for a real potential and its complex conjugate are

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            i\hbar\pdv{\Psi}{t} &= -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\pdv{^2\Psi}{x^2} + V(x)\Psi
            \\
            -i\hbar\pdv{\Psi^*}{t} &= -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\pdv{^2\Psi^*}{x^2} + V(x)\Psi^*.
        \end{aligned}

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \pdv{|\Psi|^2}{t} &= \Psi^*\pdv{\Psi}{t} + \Psi\pdv{\Psi^*}{t}
            \\
            &= \frac{i\hbar}{2m}\left(\Psi^*\pdv{^2\Psi}{x^2}-\Psi\pdv{^2\Psi^*}{x^2}\right)
            \\
            &= -\pdv{}{x}\left[\frac{i\hbar}{2m}\left(\Psi\pdv{\Psi^*}{x}-\Psi^*\pdv{\Psi}{x}\right)\right]
            \\
            &= -\pdv{J}{x}.
        \end{aligned}

    Integrating this from `a` to `b` gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \dv{P_{ab}}{t} &= \dv{t}\int_a^b|\Psi|^2\,\dd x
            \\
            &= -\int_a^b\pdv{J}{x}\,\dd x = J(a,t)-J(b,t).
        \end{aligned}

    In one dimension, the probability interpretation implies that `\Psi` has dimensions `\mathbf{L}^{-1/2}`. Since `\hbar/m` has dimensions `\mathbf{L}^2\mathbf{T}^{-1}`,

    .. math::
        [J] = \mathbf{L}^2\mathbf{T}^{-1}\mathbf{L}^{-1/2}\mathbf{L}^{-3/2} = \mathbf{T}^{-1}.

b.  The wave function in :ref:`notes/griffithsqm/chap1/1-9:Problem 1.9` is

    .. math::
        \Psi = Ae^{-amx^2/\hbar}e^{-iat},

    and

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \pdv{\Psi}{x} &= -\frac{2amx}{\hbar}\Psi
            \\
            \pdv{\Psi^*}{x} &= -\frac{2amx}{\hbar}\Psi^*.
        \end{aligned}

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        J(x,t) = 0.
