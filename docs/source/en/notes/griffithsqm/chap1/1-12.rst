Problem 1.12
======================

a.  For the harmonic oscillator in part (b) of :ref:`notes/griffithsqm/chap1/1-11:Problem 1.11`, take the initial condition to be `x(0)=A`. Its momentum can then be written as

    .. math::
        p(t) = -m\omega A\sin(\theta),\quad \theta = \omega t.

    In particular, define its maximum magnitude by

    .. math::
        p_0 \coloneqq m\omega A = \sqrt{2mE}.

    The probability of finding the particle within the range `\dd p` equals the fraction of one period `2\pi` that the particle spends within the interval `\dd\theta`. However, each value of `p` corresponds to two values of `\theta` during one period, so

    .. math::
        \rho(p)\,\dd p = \frac{2\dd\theta}{2\pi} = \frac{\dd\theta}{\pi}.

    Since `\dv{p}{\theta} = -p_0\cos(\theta)`,

    .. math::
        \rho(p) = \frac{1}{\pi}\dv{\theta}{p} = \frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{p_0^2-p^2}}.

    Therefore, Equation (1.43) becomes

    .. math::
        \rho(p) = \begin{cases}
            \frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{p_0^2-p^2}} & (|p|<p_0) \\
            0 & (|p|\ge p_0).
        \end{cases}

    This is normalized in the same way as the result in part (b) of :ref:`notes/griffithsqm/chap1/1-11:Problem 1.11`.

b.  Since `\rho(p)` is an even function,

    .. math::
        \braket{p} = 0.

    Changing the variable of integration to `p=p_0\sin\theta` gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \braket{p^2} &= \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-p_0}^{p_0}\frac{p^2\,\dd p}{\sqrt{p_0^2-p^2}}
            \\
            &= \frac{p_0^2}{\pi}\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\sin^2\theta\,\dd\theta = \frac{p_0^2}{2} = mE.
        \end{aligned}

    Therefore, the standard deviation is

    .. math::
        \sigma_p = \sqrt{\braket{p^2}-\braket{p}^2} = \sqrt{mE}.

c.  From the result in part (b) of :ref:`notes/griffithsqm/chap1/1-11:Problem 1.11`,

    .. math::
        \sigma_x\sigma_p = \sqrt{\frac{E}{k}}\sqrt{mE} = \frac{E}{\omega}.

    In quantum mechanics, `E\ge \hbar\omega/2`, so

    .. math::
        \sigma_x\sigma_p \ge \frac{\hbar}{2}.

    This is precisely the Heisenberg uncertainty principle.
