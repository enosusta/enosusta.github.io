Problem 1.15
======================

Define the inner product of the two solutions `\Psi_1,\Psi_2` by

.. math::
    I(t) \coloneqq \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Psi_1^*(x,t)\Psi_2(x,t)\,\dd x.

These wave functions satisfy the time-dependent Schrödinger equation for the same real potential `V(x)`, so

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        \pdv{\Psi_2}{t} &= \frac{i\hbar}{2m}\pdv{^2\Psi_2}{x^2}-\frac{i}{\hbar}V\Psi_2
        \\
        \pdv{\Psi_1^*}{t} &= -\frac{i\hbar}{2m}\pdv{^2\Psi_1^*}{x^2}+\frac{i}{\hbar}V\Psi_1^*.
    \end{aligned}

Therefore,

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        \dv{I}{t} &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\pdv{\Psi_1^*}{t}\Psi_2 + \Psi_1^*\pdv{\Psi_2}{t}\right)\,\dd x
        \\
        &= \frac{i\hbar}{2m}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\Psi_1^*\pdv{^2\Psi_2}{x^2}-\pdv{^2\Psi_1^*}{x^2}\Psi_2\right)\,\dd x
        \\
        &= \frac{i\hbar}{2m}\left[\Psi_1^*\pdv{\Psi_2}{x}-\pdv{\Psi_1^*}{x}\Psi_2\right]_{-\infty}^{\infty}.
    \end{aligned}

Normalizable wave functions ordinarily vanish at infinity, and hence

.. math::
    \dv{I}{t} = 0.
