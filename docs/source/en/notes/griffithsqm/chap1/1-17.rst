Problem 1.17
======================

a.  The time-dependent Schrödinger equation for the complex potential `V=V_0-i\Gamma` and its complex conjugate are

    .. math::
        i\hbar\pdv{\Psi}{t} &= -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\pdv{^2\Psi}{x^2} + (V_0-i\Gamma)\Psi
        \\
        -i\hbar\pdv{\Psi^*}{t} &= -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\pdv{^2\Psi^*}{x^2} + (V_0+i\Gamma)\Psi^*.

    Repeating the calculation in :ref:`notes/griffithsqm/chap1/1-14:Problem 1.14`, the imaginary parts of the potential no longer cancel, and we obtain

    .. math::
        \pdv{}{t}|\Psi|^2 = -\pdv{J}{x} - \frac{2\Gamma}{\hbar}|\Psi|^2.

    Integrating this over all space gives

    .. math::
        \dv{P}{t} = -[J(x,t)]_{-\infty}^{\infty} - \frac{2\Gamma}{\hbar}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\Psi|^2\,\dd x.

    For a normalizable state, the probability current vanishes at infinity, so

    .. math::
        \dv{P}{t} = -\frac{2\Gamma}{\hbar}P.

b.  Separating variables in this differential equation gives

    .. math::
        \frac{\dd P}{P} = -\frac{2\Gamma}{\hbar}\,\dd t.

    Integrating yields

    .. math::
        \ln P = -\frac{2\Gamma}{\hbar}t + C,

    where `C` is a constant of integration. Therefore,

    .. math::
        P(t) = P(0)\exp\left(-\frac{2\Gamma}{\hbar}t\right).

    If the initial state is normalized, `P(0)=1`. Comparing this with `P(t)=e^{-t/\tau}`, the “lifetime” `\tau` is

    .. math::
        \tau = \frac{\hbar}{2\Gamma}.
