Problem 1.1
================

a.  Using the number of people of each age as weights, we obtain

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \braket{j^2} &= \frac{14^2 + 15^2 + 3\cdot 16^2 + 2\cdot 22^2 + 2\cdot 24^2 + 5\cdot 25^2}{14}
            \\
            &= \frac{6434}{14} = \frac{3217}{7}.
        \end{aligned}

    Since `\braket{j} = 21`,

    .. math::
        \braket{j}^2 = 21^2 = 441.

b.  The deviation from the mean for each age is

    +------------+------+-----+-----+-----+-----+-----+
    | `j`        | 14   | 15  | 16  | 22  | 24  | 25  |
    +------------+------+-----+-----+-----+-----+-----+
    | `\Delta j` | -7   | -6  | -5  | 1   | 3   | 4   |
    +------------+------+-----+-----+-----+-----+-----+

    Therefore, the variance is

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \sigma^2 &= \frac{(-7)^2 + (-6)^2 + 3\cdot (-5)^2 + 2\cdot 1^2 + 2\cdot 3^2 + 5\cdot 4^2}{14}
            \\
            &= \frac{260}{14} = \frac{130}{7}.
        \end{aligned}

    Hence, the standard deviation is

    .. math::
        \sigma = \sqrt{\frac{130}{7}}.

c.  From (a),

    .. math::
        \sigma = \sqrt{\braket{j^2} - \braket{j}^2} = \sqrt{\frac{3217}{7} - 441} = \sqrt{\frac{130}{7}}.

    This agrees with the result in (b).
