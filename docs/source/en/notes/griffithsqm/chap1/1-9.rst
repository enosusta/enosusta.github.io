Problem 1.9
==================

a.  The normalization condition gives

    .. math::
        1 = A^2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-2amx^2/\hbar}\,\dd x = A^2\sqrt{\frac{\pi\hbar}{2am}}.

    Since `A` is positive,

    .. math::
        A = \left(\frac{2am}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}.

b.  Differentiating the wave function gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \pdv{\Psi}{t} &= -ia\Psi
            \\
            \pdv{\Psi}{x} &= -\frac{2am}{\hbar}x\Psi
            \\
            \pdv{^2\Psi}{x^2} &= \left(-\frac{2am}{\hbar} + \frac{4a^2m^2}{\hbar^2}x^2\right)\Psi.
        \end{aligned}

    Thus, the left-hand side of the time-dependent Schrödinger equation is

    .. math::
        i\hbar\pdv{\Psi}{t} = a\hbar\Psi.

    Meanwhile, the kinetic-energy term is

    .. math::
        -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\pdv{^2\Psi}{x^2} = (a\hbar - 2ma^2x^2)\Psi.

    Therefore, `\Psi` satisfies the time-dependent Schrödinger equation if the potential has the harmonic-oscillator form

    .. math::
        V(x) = 2ma^2x^2.

c.  Since the probability density `|\Psi|^2 = A^2e^{-2amx^2/\hbar}` is an even function of `x`,

    .. math::
        \braket{x} = 0.

    Moreover,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \braket{x^2} &= A^2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^2e^{-2amx^2/\hbar}\,\dd x
            \\
            &= A^2\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\left(\frac{\hbar}{2am}\right)^{3/2} = \frac{\hbar}{4am}.
        \end{aligned}

    For the momentum,

    .. math::
        \hat{p}\Psi = -i\hbar\pdv{\Psi}{x} = 2iamx\Psi,

    so

    .. math::
        \braket{p} = 0.

    Also,

    .. math::
        \hat{p}^2\Psi = -\hbar^2\pdv{^2\Psi}{x^2} = (2am\hbar - 4a^2m^2x^2)\Psi,

    and hence

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \braket{p^2} &= 2am\hbar - 4a^2m^2\braket{x^2}
            \\
            &= 2am\hbar - 4a^2m^2\frac{\hbar}{4am} = am\hbar.
        \end{aligned}

d.  From (c), the two standard deviations are

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \sigma_x &= \sqrt{\braket{x^2} - \braket{x}^2} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{4am}} = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{am}}
            \\
            \sigma_p &= \sqrt{\braket{p^2} - \braket{p}^2} = \sqrt{am\hbar}.
        \end{aligned}

    Their product is

    .. math::
        \sigma_x\sigma_p = \frac{\hbar}{2},

    which is exactly equal to the lower bound imposed by the uncertainty principle.
