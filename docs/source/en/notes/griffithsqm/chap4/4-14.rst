Problem 4.14
==================

a.  Expanding the defining expression using Leibniz's rule gives

    .. math::
        L_q(x)
        =\sum_{j=0}^q
        (-1)^j\binom{q}{j}\frac{x^j}{j!}.

    Therefore, the first four polynomials are

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            L_0(x)&=1,
            \\
            L_1(x)&=1-x,
            \\
            L_2(x)&=1-2x+\frac{x^2}{2},
            \\
            L_3(x)&=1-3x+\frac{3x^2}{2}-\frac{x^3}{6}.
        \end{aligned}

b.  For `n=5` and `l=2`,

    .. math::
        n-l-1=2,
        \quad
        2l+1=5,

    so

    .. math::
        v(\rho)=L_2^5(2\rho).

    Using the definition of the Laguerre polynomials and

    .. math::
        L_q^p(x)=(-1)^p\left(\dv{}{x}\right)^pL_{p+q}(x),

    we find

    .. math::
        L_2^5(x)
        =21-7x+\frac{x^2}{2}.

    Therefore, apart from the normalization constant,

    .. math::
        v(\rho)=21-14\rho+2\rho^2.

c.  Substituting `n=5` and `l=2` into the recurrence relation gives

    .. math::
        c_{j+1}
        =
        \frac{2(j-2)}
        {(j+1)(j+6)}c_j.

    It follows that

    .. math::
        c_1=-\frac{2}{3}c_0,
        \quad
        c_2=\frac{2}{21}c_0,
        \quad
        c_3=0.

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        v(\rho)
        =c_0\left(
            1-\frac{2}{3}\rho+\frac{2}{21}\rho^2
        \right).

    Choosing `c_0=21` gives

    .. math::
        v(\rho)=21-14\rho+2\rho^2,

    in agreement with the result in part (b).
