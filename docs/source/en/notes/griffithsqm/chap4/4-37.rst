Problem 4.37
==================

a.  The total lowering operator is `S_-=S_-^{(1)}+S_-^{(2)}`. Therefore,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            S_-\ket{1\,0}
            &=
            \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
            \left(S_-^{(1)}+S_-^{(2)}\right)
            \left(
            \ket{\uparrow\downarrow}
            +\ket{\downarrow\uparrow}
            \right)
            \\
            &=
            \frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2}}
            \left(
            \ket{\downarrow\downarrow}
            +\ket{\downarrow\downarrow}
            \right)
            \\
            &=
            \sqrt{2}\hbar\ket{1\,(-1)},
        \end{aligned}

    in agreement with the general formula for the ladder operators.

b.  Applying the raising operator to the singlet state gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            S_+\ket{0\,0}
            &=
            \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
            \left(S_+^{(1)}+S_+^{(2)}\right)
            \left(
            \ket{\uparrow\downarrow}
            -\ket{\downarrow\uparrow}
            \right)
            \\
            &=
            \frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2}}
            \left(
            \ket{\uparrow\uparrow}
            -\ket{\uparrow\uparrow}
            \right)
            =0.
        \end{aligned}

    Similarly,

    .. math::
        S_-\ket{0\,0}
        =
        \frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2}}
        \left(
        \ket{\downarrow\downarrow}
        -\ket{\downarrow\downarrow}
        \right)
        =0.

c.  The state `\ket{1\,1}=\ket{\uparrow\uparrow}` is at the top of the ladder, so `S_+\ket{1\,1}=0`, and `S_z\ket{1\,1}=\hbar\ket{1\,1}`. Using the identity

    .. math::
        S^2=S_-S_++S_z^2+\hbar S_z

    gives

    .. math::
        S^2\ket{1\,1}
        =
        2\hbar^2\ket{1\,1}.

    On the other hand, `\ket{1\,(-1)}=\ket{\downarrow\downarrow}` is at the bottom of the ladder, so `S_-\ket{1\,(-1)}=0`, and `S_z\ket{1\,(-1)}=-\hbar\ket{1\,(-1)}`. Using

    .. math::
        S^2=S_+S_-+S_z^2-\hbar S_z

    then gives

    .. math::
        S^2\ket{1\,(-1)}
        =
        2\hbar^2\ket{1\,(-1)}.

    Both states have the eigenvalue `s(s+1)\hbar^2=2\hbar^2` corresponding to `s=1`.
