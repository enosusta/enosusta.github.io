Problem 4.40
==================

a.  Let the first particle have spin 1 and the second particle have spin 2. Expanding the coupled state with `s=3` and `m=1` in terms of Clebsch--Gordan coefficients gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \ket{3\,1}
            ={}&
            \frac{1}{\sqrt{15}}
            \ket{1\,(-1)}\ket{2\,2}
            \\
            &+
            \sqrt{\frac{8}{15}}
            \ket{1\,0}\ket{2\,1}
            +
            \sqrt{\frac{2}{5}}
            \ket{1\,1}\ket{2\,0}.
        \end{aligned}

    Indeed, only the three product states satisfying `m_1+m_2=1` appear, and the sum of the squared coefficients is

    .. math::
        \frac{1}{15}+\frac{8}{15}+\frac{2}{5}=1.

    Therefore, the possible results and probabilities for measuring `S_z` of the spin-2 particle are

    .. math::
        \begin{array}{c|c}
            S_z^{(2)}&P\\ \hline
            2\hbar&\dfrac{1}{15}\\[2mm]
            \hbar&\dfrac{8}{15}\\[2mm]
            0&\dfrac{2}{5}
        \end{array}.

b.  In `\psi_{510}`, `l=1` and `m_l=0`. Since the spin points down, `s=1/2` and `m_s=-1/2`. Expanding this uncoupled state in eigenstates of the total angular momentum `\bm{J}=\bm{L}+\bm{S}` gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            &\ket{l=1,m_l=0}
            \ket{s=\frac{1}{2},m_s=-\frac{1}{2}}
            \\
            &\quad=
            \sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}
            \ket{j=\frac{3}{2},m_j=-\frac{1}{2}}
            +
            \sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}
            \ket{j=\frac{1}{2},m_j=-\frac{1}{2}}.
        \end{aligned}

    Since the eigenvalues of `J^2` are `j(j+1)\hbar^2`, the possible measurement results and their probabilities are

    .. math::
        \begin{array}{c|c}
            J^2&P\\ \hline
            \dfrac{15}{4}\hbar^2&\dfrac{2}{3}\\[2mm]
            \dfrac{3}{4}\hbar^2&\dfrac{1}{3}
        \end{array}.

    The principal quantum number `n=5` does not affect the result of coupling the angular momenta.
