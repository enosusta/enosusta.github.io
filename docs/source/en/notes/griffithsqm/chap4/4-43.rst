Problem 4.43
==================

a.  The given potentials can be written as

    .. math::
        \bm{A}
        =
        \left(
        -\frac{B_0y}{2},
        \frac{B_0x}{2},
        0
        \right),
        \quad
        \varphi=Kz^2,

    and they are independent of time. Therefore,

    .. math::
        \bm{E}
        =
        -\bm{\nabla}\varphi
        =
        -2Kz\,\hat{\bm{k}},
        \quad
        \bm{B}
        =
        \bm{\nabla}\times\bm{A}
        =
        B_0\hat{\bm{k}}.

b.  The Hamiltonian separates as

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            H
            ={}&
            \frac{1}{2m}
            \left[
            \left(p_x+\frac{qB_0}{2}y\right)^2
            +
            \left(p_y-\frac{qB_0}{2}x\right)^2
            +p_z^2
            \right]
            +qKz^2
            \\
            ={}&
            \left[
            \frac{p_x^2+p_y^2}{2m}
            +\frac{q^2B_0^2}{8m}
            \left(x^2+y^2\right)
            -\frac{qB_0}{2m}L_z
            \right]
            +
            \left(
            \frac{p_z^2}{2m}+qKz^2
            \right).
        \end{aligned}

    Define

    .. math::
        \omega_1\coloneqq\frac{qB_0}{m},
        \quad
        \omega_2\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{2qK}{m}}.

    The first two terms in the square brackets for the `x`-`y` part form a two-dimensional isotropic harmonic oscillator with frequency `\omega_1/2`. Let its radial quantum number be `n_\rho` and the quantum number of `L_z` be `m_l`. Then

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            E_{xy}
            &=
            \frac{\hbar\omega_1}{2}
            \left(
            2n_\rho+|m_l|+1-m_l
            \right)
            \\
            &=
            \hbar\omega_1
            \left(n_1+\frac{1}{2}\right),
            \\
            n_1
            &\coloneqq
            n_\rho+\frac{|m_l|-m_l}{2}
            =0,1,2,\ldots.
        \end{aligned}

    The `z` part is a one-dimensional harmonic oscillator with frequency `\omega_2`, so

    .. math::
        E_z
        =
        \hbar\omega_2
        \left(n_2+\frac{1}{2}\right),
        \quad
        n_2=0,1,2,\ldots.

    Therefore, the total energy is

    .. math::
        E(n_1,n_2)
        =
        \left(n_1+\frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar\omega_1
        +
        \left(n_2+\frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar\omega_2.

    Here, we have assumed `qB_0>0` and `qK>0`. For a general sign, the cyclotron frequency should be taken as `\omega_1=|qB_0|/m`.
