Problem 4.23
==================

a.  Write `L_i=\sum_{jk}\epsilon_{ijk}r_jp_k`. Since `\bm{L}` has no explicit time dependence, the generalized Ehrenfest theorem gives

    .. math::
        \dv{}{t}\braket{L_i}
        =
        \frac{i}{\hbar}\braket{[H,L_i]}.

    The kinetic energy commutes with `\bm{L}`, and

    .. math::
        [V,p_k]
        =i\hbar\pdv{V}{r_k}.

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            [V,L_i]
            &=
            \sum_{jk}\epsilon_{ijk}
            [V,r_jp_k]
            \\
            &=
            i\hbar
            \sum_{jk}\epsilon_{ijk}
            r_j\pdv{V}{r_k}.
        \end{aligned}

    It follows that

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \dv{}{t}\braket{L_i}
            &=
            -\left\langle
            \sum_{jk}\epsilon_{ijk}
            r_j\pdv{V}{r_k}
            \right\rangle
            \\
            &=
            \braket{\left[\bm{r}\times
            \left(-\bm{\nabla}V\right)\right]_i}
            =\braket{N_i}.
        \end{aligned}

    Combining the three components gives

    .. math::
        \dv{}{t}\braket{\bm{L}}
        =\braket{\bm{N}},
        \quad
        \bm{N}
        =\bm{r}\times\left(-\bm{\nabla}V\right).

b.  For a spherically symmetric potential `V=V(r)`,

    .. math::
        \bm{\nabla}V
        =\dv{V}{r}\hat{\bm{r}}
        =\frac{1}{r}\dv{V}{r}\bm{r},

    so the force is parallel to `\bm{r}`. Therefore,

    .. math::
        \bm{N}
        =-\frac{1}{r}\dv{V}{r}
        \left(\bm{r}\times\bm{r}\right)
        =0,

    and the result of part (a) gives

    .. math::
        \dv{}{t}\braket{\bm{L}}=0.
