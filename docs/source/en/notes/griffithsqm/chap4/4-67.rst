Problem 4.67
==================

a.  Expanding the square of the total spin `\bm{S}=\bm{S}_1+\bm{S}_2+\bm{S}_3` gives

    .. math::
        S^2
        =
        \sum_{i=1}^3S_i^2
        +2\left(
        \bm{S}_1\cdot\bm{S}_2
        +\bm{S}_2\cdot\bm{S}_3
        +\bm{S}_3\cdot\bm{S}_1
        \right).

    Since `S_i^2=3\hbar^2/4` for each particle, the Hamiltonian can be written as

    .. math::
        H
        =
        \frac{J}{2}
        \left(S^2-\frac{9}{4}\hbar^2\right).

b.  Three spin-`1/2` particles give one quartet with total spin `s=3/2` and two doublets with `s=1/2`. The energy is

    .. math::
        E_s
        =
        \frac{J\hbar^2}{2}
        \left[
        s\left(s+1\right)-\frac{9}{4}
        \right],

    so

    .. math::
        E_{3/2}=\frac{3}{4}J\hbar^2,
        \quad
        E_{1/2}=-\frac{3}{4}J\hbar^2.

    Since `J>0`, the ground-state energy is `-3J\hbar^2/4`. There are two `s=1/2` doublets, so the degeneracy is 4.

c.  Define `\bm{A}\coloneqq\bm{S}_1+\bm{S}_3` and `\bm{B}\coloneqq\bm{S}_2+\bm{S}_4`. Then `\bm{S}=\bm{A}+\bm{B}`, and

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            S^2-A^2-B^2
            &=2\bm{A}\cdot\bm{B}
            \\
            &=2\left(
            \bm{S}_1\cdot\bm{S}_2
            +\bm{S}_2\cdot\bm{S}_3
            +\bm{S}_3\cdot\bm{S}_4
            +\bm{S}_4\cdot\bm{S}_1
            \right).
        \end{aligned}

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        H
        =
        \frac{J}{2}
        \left[
        S^2-\left(\bm{S}_1+\bm{S}_3\right)^2
        -\left(\bm{S}_2+\bm{S}_4\right)^2
        \right].

    If `s_{13}` and `s_{24}` denote the spin quantum numbers of the two diagonal pairs, then

    .. math::
        E
        =
        \frac{J\hbar^2}{2}
        \left[
        s\left(s+1\right)
        -s_{13}\left(s_{13}+1\right)
        -s_{24}\left(s_{24}+1\right)
        \right].

    The minimum occurs when `s_{13}=s_{24}=1` are coupled to total spin `s=0`, giving

    .. math::
        E_0=-2J\hbar^2.

    There is only one singlet in this `1\otimes1` product, so the ground state is unique.
