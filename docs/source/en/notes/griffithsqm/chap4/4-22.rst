Problem 4.22
==================

a.  Using the commutator of a product, for example,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            [L_z,x]
            &=[xp_y-yp_x,x]
            \\
            &=-y[p_x,x]
            =i\hbar y,
            \\
            [L_z,p_x]
            &=[x,p_x]p_y
            =i\hbar p_y.
        \end{aligned}

    Similar calculations for the other components give

    .. math::
        \begin{gathered}
            [L_z,x]=i\hbar y,
            \quad
            [L_z,y]=-i\hbar x,
            \quad
            [L_z,z]=0,
            \\
            [L_z,p_x]=i\hbar p_y,
            \quad
            [L_z,p_y]=-i\hbar p_x,
            \quad
            [L_z,p_z]=0.
        \end{gathered}

b.  From part (a),

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            [L_z,L_x]
            &=[L_z,y]p_z+y[L_z,p_z]
            -[L_z,z]p_y-z[L_z,p_y]
            \\
            &=-i\hbar xp_z+i\hbar zp_x
            \\
            &=i\hbar L_y.
        \end{aligned}

c.  Using the commutation relations from part (a),

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            [L_z,r^2]
            &=[L_z,x^2]+[L_z,y^2]+[L_z,z^2]
            \\
            &=i\hbar\left(yx+xy\right)
            -i\hbar\left(xy+yx\right)
            =0,
            \\
            [L_z,p^2]
            &=[L_z,p_x^2]+[L_z,p_y^2]+[L_z,p_z^2]
            \\
            &=i\hbar\left(p_yp_x+p_xp_y\right)
            -i\hbar\left(p_xp_y+p_yp_x\right)
            =0.
        \end{aligned}

d.  Since `V=V(r)` can be regarded as a function of `r^2`, it follows from `[L_z,r^2]=0` that

    .. math::
        [L_z,V(r)]=0.

    Also, part (c) gives `[L_z,p^2]=0`, so

    .. math::
        [L_z,H]
        =\frac{1}{2m}[L_z,p^2]+[L_z,V(r)]
        =0.

    By cyclically permuting `x`, `y`, and `z`, the same argument gives

    .. math::
        [H,L_x]=[H,L_y]=[H,L_z]=0.

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        [H,L^2]
        =
        \sum_{j=x,y,z}[H,L_j^2]
        =0.
