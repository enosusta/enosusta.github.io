Problem 4.2
==================

a.  Inside the well, `V=0`, so the time-independent Schrödinger equation is

    .. math::
        -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}
        \left(
            \pdv{^2\psi}{x^2}
            +\pdv{^2\psi}{y^2}
            +\pdv{^2\psi}{z^2}
        \right)
        =E\psi.

    Setting `\psi(x,y,z)=X(x)Y(y)Z(z)` and separating variables, each factor satisfies the equation for a one-dimensional infinite square well. Imposing the boundary conditions

    .. math::
        X(0)=X(a)=Y(0)=Y(a)=Z(0)=Z(a)=0

    and normalizing, the spatial wave function is

    .. math::
        \psi_{n_xn_yn_z}(x,y,z)
        =\left(\frac{2}{a}\right)^{3/2}
        \sin\left(\frac{n_x\pi x}{a}\right)
        \sin\left(\frac{n_y\pi y}{a}\right)
        \sin\left(\frac{n_z\pi z}{a}\right),

    where

    .. math::
        n_x,n_y,n_z=1,2,3,\ldots.

    The corresponding energy is

    .. math::
        E_{n_xn_yn_z}
        =\frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{2ma^2}
        \left(n_x^2+n_y^2+n_z^2\right),

    and the complete stationary state can be written as

    .. math::
        \Psi_{n_xn_yn_z}(x,y,z,t)
        =\psi_{n_xn_yn_z}(x,y,z)e^{-iE_{n_xn_yn_z}t/\hbar}.

b.  Define the unit of energy by

    .. math::
        \varepsilon\coloneqq\frac{\pi^2\hbar^2}{2ma^2}.

    The six lowest distinct energies, together with the corresponding sets of quantum numbers and their degeneracies, are

    .. math::
        \begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
            k&E_k/\varepsilon
            &(n_x,n_y,n_z)\ \mathrm{up\ to\ permutations}
            &\mathrm{degeneracy}
            \\\hline
            1&3 &(1,1,1)&1\\
            2&6 &(1,1,2)&3\\
            3&9 &(1,2,2)&3\\
            4&11&(1,1,3)&3\\
            5&12&(2,2,2)&1\\
            6&14&(1,2,3)&6
        \end{array}.

c.  The distinct energy levels begin as

    .. math::
        \frac{E_k}{\varepsilon}
        =3,6,9,11,12,14,17,18,19,21,22,24,26,27,\ldots,

    so the fourteenth distinct energy is

    .. math::
        E_{14}=27\varepsilon
        =\frac{27\pi^2\hbar^2}{2ma^2}.

    Since

    .. math::
        27=1^2+1^2+5^2=3^2+3^2+3^2,

    the four states are

    .. math::
        (1,1,5),
        \quad
        (1,5,1),
        \quad
        (5,1,1),
        \quad
        (3,3,3),

    and the degeneracy is four. The degeneracy of the first three states arises from the symmetry under interchange of the coordinate axes of the cube, but `(3,3,3)` cannot be obtained from them by a permutation. Thus, this level includes an accidental degeneracy that cannot be explained immediately by symmetry alone.
