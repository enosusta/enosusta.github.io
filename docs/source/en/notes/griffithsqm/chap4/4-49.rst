Problem 4.49
==================

a.  The time-dependent Schrödinger equation and its complex conjugate are

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            i\hbar\pdv{\Psi}{t}
            &=
            -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\Psi
            +V\Psi,
            \\
            -i\hbar\pdv{\Psi^*}{t}
            &=
            -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\Psi^*
            +V\Psi^*.
        \end{aligned}

    Here, `V` is assumed to be real. These equations give

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \pdv{}{t}|\Psi|^2
            &=
            \Psi^*\pdv{\Psi}{t}
            +\Psi\pdv{\Psi^*}{t}
            \\
            &=
            \frac{i\hbar}{2m}
            \left(
            \Psi^*\nabla^2\Psi
            -\Psi\nabla^2\Psi^*
            \right).
        \end{aligned}

    On the other hand, the divergence of the probability current is

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \bm{\nabla}\cdot\bm{J}
            &=
            \frac{i\hbar}{2m}
            \bm{\nabla}\cdot
            \left(
            \Psi\bm{\nabla}\Psi^*
            -\Psi^*\bm{\nabla}\Psi
            \right)
            \\
            &=
            \frac{i\hbar}{2m}
            \left(
            \Psi\nabla^2\Psi^*
            -\Psi^*\nabla^2\Psi
            \right)
            \\
            &=
            -\pdv{}{t}|\Psi|^2.
        \end{aligned}

    Integrating over a volume `\mathcal V` and using the divergence theorem also gives

    .. math::
        \oint_S\bm{J}\cdot\dd\bm{a}
        =
        -\dv{}{t}
        \int_{\mathcal V}|\Psi|^2\,\dd^3\bm{r}.

b.  The normalized spatial wave function is

    .. math::
        \psi_{211}
        =
        -\frac{1}{8\sqrt{\pi}a^{3/2}}
        \left(\frac{r}{a}\right)
        e^{-r/(2a)}
        \sin\theta\,e^{i\phi}.

    Since every factor except the `\phi` dependence is real,

    .. math::
        \bm{J}
        =
        \frac{\hbar}{m}
        \operatorname{Im}
        \left(\psi_{211}^*\bm{\nabla}\psi_{211}\right)
        =
        \frac{\hbar}{m}
        \frac{|\psi_{211}|^2}{r\sin\theta}
        \hat{\bm{\phi}}.

    Substituting `|\psi_{211}|^2` gives

    .. math::
        \bm{J}
        =
        \frac{\hbar}{64\pi ma^5}
        re^{-r/a}\sin\theta\,
        \hat{\bm{\phi}}.

c.  The azimuthal current is axially symmetric, so the `x` and `y` components of `\bm{L}` vanish upon integration. Also,

    .. math::
        \left(\bm{r}\times\bm{J}\right)_z
        =
        rJ_\phi\sin\theta.

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            L_z
            &=
            m\int
            rJ_\phi\sin\theta\,
            r^2\sin\theta\,
            \dd r\,\dd\theta\,\dd\phi
            \\
            &=
            \frac{\hbar}{64\pi a^5}
            \left(
            \int_0^\infty r^4e^{-r/a}\,\dd r
            \right)
            \left(
            \int_0^\pi\sin^3\theta\,\dd\theta
            \right)
            \left(
            \int_0^{2\pi}\dd\phi
            \right)
            \\
            &=
            \frac{\hbar}{64\pi a^5}
            \left(24a^5\right)
            \left(\frac{4}{3}\right)
            \left(2\pi\right)
            =\hbar.
        \end{aligned}

    This agrees with the fact that `\psi_{211}` has the `L_z` eigenvalue `m_l\hbar=\hbar`.
