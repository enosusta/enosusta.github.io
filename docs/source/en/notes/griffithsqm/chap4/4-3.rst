Problem 4.3
==================

a.  Since the wave function is independent of the angles, the Laplacian in spherical coordinates is

    .. math::
        \nabla^2\psi
        =\frac{1}{r^2}\dv{}{r}
        \left(r^2\dv{\psi}{r}\right).

    For `\psi=Ae^{-r/a}`,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \dv{\psi}{r}&=-\frac{1}{a}\psi,
            \\
            \nabla^2\psi
            &=\left(\frac{1}{a^2}-\frac{2}{ar}\right)\psi.
        \end{aligned}

    Therefore, the time-independent Schrödinger equation gives

    .. math::
        V(r)
        =E+\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\nabla^2\psi}{\psi}
        =E+\frac{\hbar^2}{2ma^2}
        -\frac{\hbar^2}{mar}.

    For `V(r)\to0` as `r\to\infty`, we must have

    .. math::
        E=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2ma^2}.

    Hence,

    .. math::
        V(r)=-\frac{\hbar^2}{mar}.

b.  This time, `\psi=Ae^{-r^2/a^2}`, so

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \dv{\psi}{r}&=-\frac{2r}{a^2}\psi,
            \\
            \nabla^2\psi
            &=\left(-\frac{6}{a^2}+\frac{4r^2}{a^4}\right)\psi.
        \end{aligned}

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        V(r)
        =E-\frac{3\hbar^2}{ma^2}
        +\frac{2\hbar^2}{ma^4}r^2.

    The condition `V(0)=0` gives

    .. math::
        E=\frac{3\hbar^2}{ma^2},

    and hence the required potential is

    .. math::
        V(r)=\frac{2\hbar^2}{ma^4}r^2.
