Problem 4.36
==================

a.  Since the magnetic field points only in the `z` direction,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            H(t)
            &=
            -\gamma\bm{B}(t)\cdot\bm{S}
            \\
            &=
            -\gamma B_0\cos\left(\omega t\right)S_z
            \\
            &=
            -\frac{\gamma B_0\hbar}{2}
            \cos\left(\omega t\right)
            \begin{pmatrix}
                1&0\\
                0&-1
            \end{pmatrix}.
        \end{aligned}

b.  Writing `\chi(t)=(a(t),b(t))^{\mathsf T}`, the time-dependent Schrödinger equation becomes

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            i\hbar\dv{a}{t}
            &=
            -\frac{\gamma B_0\hbar}{2}
            \cos\left(\omega t\right)a,
            \\
            i\hbar\dv{b}{t}
            &=
            \frac{\gamma B_0\hbar}{2}
            \cos\left(\omega t\right)b.
        \end{aligned}

    Integrating gives

    .. math::
        a(t)=a(0)e^{i\beta(t)},
        \quad
        b(t)=b(0)e^{-i\beta(t)},
        \quad
        \beta(t)
        \coloneqq
        \frac{\gamma B_0}{2\omega}
        \sin\left(\omega t\right).

    Since the initial state is

    .. math::
        \chi(0)
        =
        \chi_+^{(x)}
        =
        \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
        \begin{pmatrix}
            1\\
            1
        \end{pmatrix},

    we obtain

    .. math::
        \chi(t)
        =
        \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
        \begin{pmatrix}
            e^{i\beta(t)}\\
            e^{-i\beta(t)}
        \end{pmatrix}.

c.  The negative eigenspinor of `S_x` is

    .. math::
        \chi_-^{(x)}
        =
        \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
        \begin{pmatrix}
            1\\
            -1
        \end{pmatrix},

    so its probability amplitude is

    .. math::
        \left(\chi_-^{(x)}\right)^\dagger\chi(t)
        =
        \frac{1}{2}
        \left(e^{i\beta(t)}-e^{-i\beta(t)}\right)
        =
        i\sin\beta(t).

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        P_x^-(t)
        =
        \sin^2
        \left[
        \frac{\gamma B_0}{2\omega}
        \sin\left(\omega t\right)
        \right].

d.  For complete spin reversal, it is sufficient that `P_x^-=1`, or equivalently `|\beta|=\pi/2`, occur at least once. Since `|\sin(\omega t)|\leq1`, we require

    .. math::
        \frac{|\gamma|B_0}{2\omega}
        \geq\frac{\pi}{2}.

    Thus, the minimum magnetic field is

    .. math::
        B_{0,\min}
        =
        \frac{\pi\omega}{|\gamma|}.

    For an electron, using `|\gamma|=e/m` gives

    .. math::
        B_{0,\min}
        =
        \frac{\pi m\omega}{e}.
