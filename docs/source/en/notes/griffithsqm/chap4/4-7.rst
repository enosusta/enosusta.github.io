Problem 4.7
==================

The highest-degree term of `(x^2-1)^l` in Rodrigues' formula is `x^{2l}`. Therefore, the highest-degree term of `P_l(x)` is

.. math::
    P_l(x)
    =
    \frac{(2l)!}{2^l(l!)^2}x^l
    +\mathrm{lower\ powers}.

Differentiating `P_l(x)` another `l` times eliminates all the lower-degree terms, so

.. math::
    \left(\dv{}{x}\right)^l P_l(x)
    =\frac{(2l)!}{2^ll!}
    =(2l-1)!!,

and hence

.. math::
    P_l^l(x)
    =(-1)^l(2l-1)!!
    \left(1-x^2\right)^{l/2}.

Substituting this into the definition of the spherical harmonics gives

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        Y_l^l(\theta,\phi)
        &=
        (-1)^l
        \sqrt{
            \frac{2l+1}{4\pi(2l)!}
        }
        \frac{(2l)!}{2^ll!}
        \sin^l\theta\,e^{il\phi}
        \\
        &=
        \frac{(-1)^l}{2^ll!}
        \sqrt{\frac{(2l+1)!}{4\pi}}
        \sin^l\theta\,e^{il\phi}.
    \end{aligned}

Also,

.. math::
    P_3(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left(5x^3-3x\right),

so

.. math::
    P_3^2(x)
    =\left(1-x^2\right)\dv{^2P_3}{x^2}
    =15x\left(1-x^2\right),

and therefore

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        Y_3^2(\theta,\phi)
        &=
        \sqrt{
            \frac{7}{4\pi}\frac{(3-2)!}{(3+2)!}
        }
        e^{2i\phi}P_3^2(\cos\theta)
        \\
        &=
        \sqrt{\frac{105}{32\pi}}
        \sin^2\theta\cos\theta\,e^{2i\phi}.
    \end{aligned}

We now verify that these functions satisfy the angular equation. Writing the constant factor as `C_l`, let

.. math::
    Y_l^l=C_l\sin^l\theta\,e^{il\phi}.

Then

.. math::
    \pdv{Y_l^l}{\theta}
    =l\cot\theta\,Y_l^l,
    \quad
    \pdv{^2Y_l^l}{\phi^2}
    =-l^2Y_l^l,

and hence

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        &\sin\theta\pdv{}{\theta}
        \left(
            \sin\theta\pdv{Y_l^l}{\theta}
        \right)
        +\pdv{^2Y_l^l}{\phi^2}
        \\
        &\quad=
        \left[
            l\left(-\sin^2\theta+l\cos^2\theta\right)
            -l^2
        \right]Y_l^l
        \\
        &\quad=
        -l(l+1)\sin^2\theta\,Y_l^l.
    \end{aligned}

Thus, it satisfies the angular equation for `m=l`. Also, setting `Y_3^2=C\sin^2\theta\cos\theta\,e^{2i\phi}` gives

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        \sin\theta\pdv{}{\theta}
        \left(
            \sin\theta\pdv{Y_3^2}{\theta}
        \right)
        &=4\left(3\cos^2\theta-2\right)Y_3^2,
        \\
        \pdv{^2Y_3^2}{\phi^2}
        &=-4Y_3^2.
    \end{aligned}

Therefore, the sum of these two expressions is

.. math::
    -12\sin^2\theta\,Y_3^2
    =-3(3+1)\sin^2\theta\,Y_3^2.

Thus, this function also satisfies the angular equation for `l=3` and `m=2`.
