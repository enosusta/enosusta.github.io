Problem 4.18
==================

a.  The states `\psi_{211}` and `\psi_{21-1}` have the same energy

    .. math::
        E_2=\frac{E_1}{4}.

    Thus, they have a common time dependence, and

    .. math::
        \Psi(\bm{r},t)
        =
        \frac{e^{-iE_2t/\hbar}}{\sqrt{2}}
        \left(\psi_{211}+\psi_{21-1}\right).

    Substituting the wave functions from :ref:`notes/griffithsqm/chap4/4-13:Problem 4.13` gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
            \left(\psi_{211}+\psi_{21-1}\right)
            &=
            \frac{1}{8\sqrt{2\pi}a^{3/2}}
            \left(\frac{r}{a}\right)e^{-r/(2a)}
            \sin\theta
            \left(e^{-i\phi}-e^{i\phi}\right)
            \\
            &=
            -\frac{i}{4\sqrt{2\pi}a^{3/2}}
            \left(\frac{r}{a}\right)e^{-r/(2a)}
            \sin\theta\sin\phi.
        \end{aligned}

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        \Psi(\bm{r},t)
        =
        -\frac{i\,e^{-iE_2t/\hbar}}
        {4\sqrt{2\pi}a^{3/2}}
        \left(\frac{r}{a}\right)e^{-r/(2a)}
        \sin\theta\sin\phi.

b.  Since the Coulomb potential is a function of `r` alone, its expectation value is independent of the choice of normalized angular function. Using `R_{21}` gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \left\langle\frac{1}{r}\right\rangle
            &=\int_0^\infty
            |R_{21}(r)|^2r\,\dd r
            \\
            &=\frac{1}{24a^5}
            \int_0^\infty r^3e^{-r/a}\,\dd r
            \\
            &=\frac{1}{4a}.
        \end{aligned}

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        \braket{V}
        =-\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}
        \left\langle\frac{1}{r}\right\rangle
        =-\frac{e^2}{16\pi\epsilon_0a}.

    Since `e^2/(4\pi\epsilon_0a)=-2E_1`,

    .. math::
        \braket{V}
        =\frac{E_1}{2}
        =2E_2
        =-6.80\ \mathrm{eV}.

    This linear combination is a stationary state composed entirely of degenerate eigenstates with the same energy, so `\braket{V}` is independent of time.
