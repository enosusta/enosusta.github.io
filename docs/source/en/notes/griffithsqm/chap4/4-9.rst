Problem 4.9
==================

a.  Substituting `l=1` into the definition of the spherical Neumann functions gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            n_1(x)
            &=-(-x)\left(\frac{1}{x}\dv{}{x}\right)
            \frac{\cos x}{x}
            \\
            &=\dv{}{x}\left(\frac{\cos x}{x}\right)
            \\
            &=-\frac{\cos x}{x^2}-\frac{\sin x}{x}.
        \end{aligned}

    Similarly, for `l=2`,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            n_2(x)
            &=-x^2
            \left(\frac{1}{x}\dv{}{x}\right)^2
            \frac{\cos x}{x}
            \\
            &=
            \left(\frac{1}{x}-\frac{3}{x^3}\right)\cos x
            -\frac{3}{x^2}\sin x.
        \end{aligned}

b.  Substituting the expansions for `x\ll1`,

    .. math::
        \sin x
        =x-\frac{x^3}{6}+\frac{x^5}{120}+\cdots,
        \quad
        \cos x
        =1-\frac{x^2}{2}+\frac{x^4}{24}+\cdots,

    into the results of part (a) gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            n_1(x)
            &=-\frac{1}{x^2}-\frac{1}{2}
            +\frac{x^2}{8}+O(x^4),
            \\
            n_2(x)
            &=-\frac{3}{x^3}-\frac{1}{2x}
            -\frac{x}{8}+O(x^3).
        \end{aligned}

    In particular, the leading terms are

    .. math::
        n_1(x)\sim-\frac{1}{x^2},
        \quad
        n_2(x)\sim-\frac{3}{x^3}
        \quad
        (x\to0),

    and both functions diverge at the origin.
