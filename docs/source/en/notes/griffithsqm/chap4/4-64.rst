Problem 4.64
==================

a.  Both terms have `l=1`, so the only possible result is

    .. math::
        L^2=2\hbar^2,

    with probability 1.

b.  The first term has `m_l=0`, whereas the second has `m_l=1`. Therefore,

    .. math::
        \begin{array}{c|cc}
            L_z&0&\hbar\\ \hline
            P&1/3&2/3
        \end{array}.

c.  The electron spin is fixed at `s=1/2`. Hence,

    .. math::
        S^2=\frac{3}{4}\hbar^2

    is measured with probability 1.

d.  The first term is spin up, and the second is spin down, so

    .. math::
        \begin{array}{c|cc}
            S_z&\hbar/2&-\hbar/2\\ \hline
            P&1/3&2/3
        \end{array}.

e.  The coupled basis in the `m_j=1/2` subspace is

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \ket{\frac{3}{2}\,\frac{1}{2}}
            &=
            \sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}
            \ket{1\,0}\ket{\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{1}{2}}
            +
            \sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}
            \ket{1\,1}\ket{\frac{1}{2}\,{-\frac{1}{2}}},
            \\
            \ket{\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{1}{2}}
            &=
            \sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}
            \ket{1\,0}\ket{\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{1}{2}}
            -
            \sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}
            \ket{1\,1}\ket{\frac{1}{2}\,{-\frac{1}{2}}}.
        \end{aligned}

    Thus, the given angular and spin state can be written as

    .. math::
        \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}
        \ket{\frac{3}{2}\,\frac{1}{2}}
        -
        \frac{1}{3}
        \ket{\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{1}{2}}.

    It follows that

    .. math::
        \begin{array}{c|cc}
            J^2&15\hbar^2/4&3\hbar^2/4\\ \hline
            P&8/9&1/9
        \end{array}.

f.  Both terms have `m_j=m_l+m_s=1/2`, so

    .. math::
        J_z=\frac{\hbar}{2}

    is measured with probability 1.

g.  Since the spin states are orthogonal, the position probability density is

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \rho\left(r,\theta,\phi\right)
            &=
            \left|R_{21}(r)\right|^2
            \left[
            \frac{1}{3}\left|Y_1^0\right|^2
            +
            \frac{2}{3}\left|Y_1^1\right|^2
            \right]
            \\
            &=
            \frac{1}{96\pi a^3}
            \left(\frac{r}{a}\right)^2e^{-r/a}.
        \end{aligned}

    This density is isotropic because the angular dependence cancels. The probability of finding the particle in the infinitesimal volume `\dd^3\bm{r}` is `\rho\,\dd^3\bm{r}`.

h.  Retain only the spin-up component and integrate over the angles. The joint probability per unit `r` is

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \dv{P_+}{r}
            &=
            \frac{1}{3}r^2\left|R_{21}(r)\right|^2
            \int\left|Y_1^0\right|^2\dd\Omega
            \\
            &=
            \frac{r^4}{72a^5}e^{-r/a}.
        \end{aligned}
