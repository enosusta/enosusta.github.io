Problem 4.33
==================

The component of spin in the `\hat{\bm{r}}` direction is

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        S_r
        &=
        \hat{\bm{r}}\cdot\bm{S}
        \\
        &=
        S_x\sin\theta\cos\phi
        +S_y\sin\theta\sin\phi
        +S_z\cos\theta
        \\
        &=
        \frac{\hbar}{2}
        \begin{pmatrix}
            \cos\theta&
            \sin\theta\,e^{-i\phi}\\
            \sin\theta\,e^{i\phi}&
            -\cos\theta
        \end{pmatrix}.
    \end{aligned}

The characteristic equation is

.. math::
    \det\left(S_r-\lambda\right)
    =
    \lambda^2-\frac{\hbar^2}{4}
    =0,

so the eigenvalues are

.. math::
    \lambda_\pm=\pm\frac{\hbar}{2}.

For `\lambda_+=\hbar/2`,

.. math::
    \left(\cos\theta-1\right)a
    +\sin\theta\,e^{-i\phi}b=0.

The half-angle formulas therefore give `b/a=e^{i\phi}\tan(\theta/2)`, and normalization yields

.. math::
    \chi_+^{(r)}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \cos\left(\theta/2\right)\\
        e^{i\phi}\sin\left(\theta/2\right)
    \end{pmatrix}.

Similarly, a normalized eigenspinor for `\lambda_-=-\hbar/2` may be chosen as

.. math::
    \chi_-^{(r)}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        e^{-i\phi}\sin\left(\theta/2\right)\\
        -\cos\left(\theta/2\right)
    \end{pmatrix}.

Indeed,

.. math::
    S_r\chi_\pm^{(r)}
    =
    \pm\frac{\hbar}{2}\chi_\pm^{(r)},
    \quad
    \left(\chi_\pm^{(r)}\right)^\dagger
    \chi_\pm^{(r)}=1.

Each eigenspinor may also be multiplied by an arbitrary overall phase.
