Problem 4.35
==================

a.  Define `\omega_L\coloneqq\gamma B_0`. The state in Example 4.3 is

    .. math::
        \chi(t)
        =
        \begin{pmatrix}
            \cos\left(\alpha/2\right)e^{i\omega_Lt/2}\\
            \sin\left(\alpha/2\right)e^{-i\omega_Lt/2}
        \end{pmatrix},

    and the positive eigenspinor of `S_x` is

    .. math::
        \chi_+^{(x)}
        =
        \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
        \begin{pmatrix}
            1\\
            1
        \end{pmatrix}.

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            P_x^+(t)
            &=
            \left|
            \left(\chi_+^{(x)}\right)^\dagger\chi(t)
            \right|^2
            \\
            &=
            \frac{1}{2}
            \left[
            1+\sin\alpha\cos\left(\omega_Lt\right)
            \right].
        \end{aligned}

b.  Using the positive eigenspinor of `S_y`,

    .. math::
        \chi_+^{(y)}
        =
        \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
        \begin{pmatrix}
            1\\
            i
        \end{pmatrix},

    gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            P_y^+(t)
            &=
            \left|
            \left(\chi_+^{(y)}\right)^\dagger\chi(t)
            \right|^2
            \\
            &=
            \frac{1}{2}
            \left[
            1-\sin\alpha\sin\left(\omega_Lt\right)
            \right].
        \end{aligned}

c.  The positive eigenspinor of `S_z` is `\chi_+=(1,0)^{\mathsf T}`, so

    .. math::
        P_z^+(t)
        =
        \left|\chi_+^\dagger\chi(t)\right|^2
        =
        \cos^2\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)
        =
        \frac{1+\cos\alpha}{2}.

    Since the magnetic field points in the `z` direction, this probability is independent of time.
