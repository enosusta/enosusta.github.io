Problem 4.27
==================

a.  Let the distances from the center of mass to `m_1` and `m_2` be `r_1` and `r_2`, respectively. Since

    .. math::
        r_1+r_2=a,
        \quad
        m_1r_1=m_2r_2,

    we have

    .. math::
        r_1=\frac{m_2}{m_1+m_2}a,
        \quad
        r_2=\frac{m_1}{m_1+m_2}a.

    Therefore, the moment of inertia about the center of mass is

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            I
            &=m_1r_1^2+m_2r_2^2
            \\
            &=\frac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2}a^2.
        \end{aligned}

    The classical rotational energy is

    .. math::
        T=\frac{1}{2}I\omega^2
        =\frac{L^2}{2I}.

    In quantum mechanics, the eigenvalues of `L^2` are `\hbar^2n(n+1)`, so

    .. math::
        E_n
        =
        \frac{\hbar^2}{2I}n\left(n+1\right),
        \quad
        n=0,1,2,\ldots.

b.  Describing the orientation of the rotation axis by `(\theta,\phi)`, the normalized eigenfunctions are the spherical harmonics

    .. math::
        Y_n^m(\theta,\phi),
        \quad
        m=-n,-n+1,\ldots,n.

    Since the energy is independent of `m`, the degeneracy of the `n`th level is `2n+1`.

c.  The selection rule for electric dipole transitions is `\Delta n=\pm1`. For a transition from `n=j` to `n=j-1`,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            h\nu_j
            &=E_j-E_{j-1}
            \\
            &=
            \frac{\hbar^2}{2I}
            \left[
            j\left(j+1\right)-j\left(j-1\right)
            \right]
            \\
            &=\frac{\hbar^2j}{I}.
        \end{aligned}

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        \nu_j
        =
        \frac{\hbar j}{2\pi I},
        \quad
        j=1,2,3,\ldots.

    Thus, the spectral lines are equally spaced, with

    .. math::
        \Delta\nu=\frac{\hbar}{2\pi I}.

d.  From Figure 4.13, the wavenumber spacing between adjacent strong absorption lines is approximately

    .. math::
        \Delta\tilde{\nu}
        \approx3.85\ \mathrm{cm}^{-1}.

    Therefore, the frequency spacing is

    .. math::
        \Delta\nu
        =c\Delta\tilde{\nu}
        \approx1.15\times10^{11}\ \mathrm{Hz}.

    Using `\Delta\nu=\hbar/(2\pi I)` and `I=\mu a^2` gives

    .. math::
        I=\frac{\hbar}{2\pi\Delta\nu},
        \quad
        \mu=\frac{m_1m_2}{m_1+m_2},
        \quad
        a=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2\pi\mu\Delta\nu}}.

    .. exec_code::

        import math

        hbar = 1.054571817e-34
        c = 2.99792458e10
        u = 1.66053906660e-27

        delta_wavenumber = 3.85
        delta_nu = c * delta_wavenumber
        m1 = 12.0 * u
        m2 = 16.0 * u
        mu = m1 * m2 / (m1 + m2)
        I = hbar / (2.0 * math.pi * delta_nu)
        a = math.sqrt(I / mu)

        print(f"Delta nu = {delta_nu:.3e} Hz")
        print(f"I = {I:.3e} kg m^2")
        print(f"a = {a:.3e} m")
