Problem 4.70
==================

a.  For the particles whose first measurement is along `z`, the initial state is spin up along `z`, so every particle gives the up result. Regardless of whether it is retained by the subsequent measurement along `x`, its first result was up.

    For the particles whose first measurement is along `x`, that measurement leaves each particle in either the spin-up or spin-down state along `x`. Since there are no external forces or torques, the second measurement along `x` repeats the same result. Thus, every particle retained at the end must also have been up in the first measurement. For either measurement direction, the required fraction is

    .. math::
        1.

b.  Let `\theta_{ab}` be the angle between `\bm{a}` and `\bm{b}`, and let `\theta_{bc}` be the angle between `\bm{b}` and `\bm{c}`. The probabilities in the first measurement are

    .. math::
        P_+
        =
        \cos^2\left(\frac{\theta_{ab}}{2}\right),
        \quad
        P_-
        =
        \sin^2\left(\frac{\theta_{ab}}{2}\right).

    If the first result is down, the angle between that direction and `\bm{c}` is `\pi-\theta_{bc}`. Therefore, the joint probabilities for the two measurement results are

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            P_{++}
            &=
            \cos^2\left(\frac{\theta_{ab}}{2}\right)
            \cos^2\left(\frac{\theta_{bc}}{2}\right),
            \\
            P_{+-}
            &=
            \cos^2\left(\frac{\theta_{ab}}{2}\right)
            \sin^2\left(\frac{\theta_{bc}}{2}\right),
            \\
            P_{-+}
            &=
            \sin^2\left(\frac{\theta_{ab}}{2}\right)
            \sin^2\left(\frac{\theta_{bc}}{2}\right),
            \\
            P_{--}
            &=
            \sin^2\left(\frac{\theta_{ab}}{2}\right)
            \cos^2\left(\frac{\theta_{bc}}{2}\right).
        \end{aligned}

    Conditioned on the final measurement having given the up result, the probability that the first measurement also gave the up result is

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            P\left(b+\mid c+\right)
            &=
            \frac{P_{++}}{P_{++}+P_{-+}}
            \\
            &=
            \left[
            1+
            \tan^2\left(\frac{\theta_{ab}}{2}\right)
            \tan^2\left(\frac{\theta_{bc}}{2}\right)
            \right]^{-1}.
        \end{aligned}
