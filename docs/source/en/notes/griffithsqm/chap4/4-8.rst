Problem 4.8
==================

First, suppose that `l>l'`. Rodrigues' formula gives

.. math::
    \int_{-1}^1P_l(x)P_{l'}(x)\,\dd x
    =
    \frac{1}{2^ll!}
    \int_{-1}^1
    P_{l'}(x)
    \frac{\dd^l}{\dd x^l}
    \left(x^2-1\right)^l
    \,\dd x.

The function `\left(x^2-1\right)^l` and its derivatives through order `l-1` vanish at `x=\pm1`. Thus, all boundary terms vanish, and integrating by parts `l` times gives

.. math::
    \int_{-1}^1P_l P_{l'}\,\dd x
    =
    \frac{(-1)^l}{2^ll!}
    \int_{-1}^1
    \left(x^2-1\right)^l
    \frac{\dd^l P_{l'}}{\dd x^l}
    \,\dd x.

Since `P_{l'}` is a polynomial of degree `l'`, if `l>l'`, then

.. math::
    \frac{\dd^l P_{l'}}{\dd x^l}=0.

Using the symmetry of the integral as well, for `l\neq l'` we therefore have

.. math::
    \int_{-1}^1P_l(x)P_{l'}(x)\,\dd x=0.

Next, let `l=l'`. Extracting the highest-degree term of `P_l` from Rodrigues' formula gives

.. math::
    P_l(x)
    =
    \frac{(2l)!}{2^l(l!)^2}x^l
    +\mathrm{lower\ powers},

so

.. math::
    \frac{\dd^l P_l}{\dd x^l}
    =\frac{(2l)!}{2^ll!}.

Integrating by parts `l` times as before gives

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        \int_{-1}^1P_l(x)^2\,\dd x
        &=
        \frac{(-1)^l}{2^ll!}
        \int_{-1}^1
        \left(x^2-1\right)^l
        \frac{\dd^l P_l}{\dd x^l}
        \,\dd x
        \\
        &=
        \frac{(2l)!}{2^{2l}(l!)^2}
        \int_{-1}^1\left(1-x^2\right)^l\,\dd x.
    \end{aligned}

In the last integral, the substitution `x=2t-1` gives

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        \int_{-1}^1\left(1-x^2\right)^l\,\dd x
        &=2^{2l+1}
        \int_0^1t^l(1-t)^l\,\dd t
        \\
        &=2^{2l+1}
        \frac{(l!)^2}{(2l+1)!}.
    \end{aligned}

Therefore,

.. math::
    \int_{-1}^1P_l(x)^2\,\dd x
    =
    \frac{(2l)!}{2^{2l}(l!)^2}
    \frac{2^{2l+1}(l!)^2}{(2l+1)!}
    =\frac{2}{2l+1}.

Combining the two cases gives

.. math::
    \int_{-1}^1P_l(x)P_{l'}(x)\,\dd x
    =\frac{2}{2l+1}\delta_{ll'}.
