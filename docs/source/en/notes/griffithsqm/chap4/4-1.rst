Problem 4.1
==================

a.  Let `i,j\in\{x,y,z\}`, and write the position and momentum operators as

    .. math::
        (\hat{r}_if)(\bm{r})=r_if(\bm{r}),
        \quad
        (\hat{p}_jf)(\bm{r})=-i\hbar\pdv{f}{r_j}.

    Acting on an arbitrary sufficiently smooth function `f(\bm{r})` gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            [r_i,p_j]f
            &=r_i\left(-i\hbar\pdv{f}{r_j}\right)
            +i\hbar\pdv{}{r_j}(r_if)
            \\
            &=i\hbar\pdv{r_i}{r_j}f
            \\
            &=i\hbar\delta_{ij}f.
        \end{aligned}

    Also, since partial derivatives commute,

    .. math::
        [r_i,r_j]=0,
        \quad
        [p_i,p_j]=0.

    Therefore, all the canonical commutation relations are

    .. math::
        [r_i,p_j]=i\hbar\delta_{ij},
        \quad
        [p_i,r_j]=-i\hbar\delta_{ij},
        \quad
        [r_i,r_j]=[p_i,p_j]=0.

b.  Using the fact that `\hat{H}` is Hermitian in three dimensions as well, for an arbitrary operator `\hat{Q}` we have

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \dv{}{t}\braket{\hat{Q}}
            &=\int\left[
                \pdv{\Psi^*}{t}\hat{Q}\Psi
                +\Psi^*\pdv{\hat{Q}}{t}\Psi
                +\Psi^*\hat{Q}\pdv{\Psi}{t}
            \right],\dd^3\bm{r}
            \\
            &=\frac{i}{\hbar}\braket{[\hat{H},\hat{Q}]}
            +\left\langle\pdv{\hat{Q}}{t}\right\rangle.
        \end{aligned}

    This is the three-dimensional version of the generalized Ehrenfest theorem. Here, let

    .. math::
        \hat{H}=\frac{\bm{p}^{\,2}}{2m}+V(\bm{r}).

    For each component of position, the commutation relations in part (a) give

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            [\hat{H},r_i]
            &=\frac{1}{2m}\sum_j[p_j^2,r_i]
            \\
            &=\frac{1}{2m}\sum_j
            \left(p_j[p_j,r_i]+[p_j,r_i]p_j\right)
            \\
            &=-\frac{i\hbar}{m}p_i,
        \end{aligned}

    and hence

    .. math::
        \dv{}{t}\braket{r_i}
        =\frac{i}{\hbar}\braket{[\hat{H},r_i]}
        =\frac{1}{m}\braket{p_i}.

    On the other hand, for each component of momentum,

    .. math::
        [\hat{H},p_i]=[V,p_i]
        =i\hbar\pdv{V}{r_i},

    so

    .. math::
        \dv{}{t}\braket{p_i}
        =\frac{i}{\hbar}\braket{[\hat{H},p_i]}
        =-\left\langle\pdv{V}{r_i}\right\rangle.

    Combining the three components gives

    .. math::
        \dv{}{t}\braket{\bm{r}}
        =\frac{1}{m}\braket{\bm{p}},
        \quad
        \dv{}{t}\braket{\bm{p}}
        =\braket{-\bm{\nabla}V}.

c.  Applying the commutation relations in part (a) to the general uncertainty relation

    .. math::
        \sigma_A\sigma_B
        \geq\frac{1}{2}\left|\braket{[\hat{A},\hat{B}]}\right|

    gives

    .. math::
        \sigma_{r_i}\sigma_{p_j}
        \geq\frac{\hbar}{2}\delta_{ij}.

    Therefore, the three nontrivial relations are

    .. math::
        \sigma_x\sigma_{p_x}\geq\frac{\hbar}{2},
        \quad
        \sigma_y\sigma_{p_y}\geq\frac{\hbar}{2},
        \quad
        \sigma_z\sigma_{p_z}\geq\frac{\hbar}{2}.

    Position and momentum in different directions commute, so, for example, the commutation relations provide no positive lower bound on `\sigma_x\sigma_{p_y}`.
