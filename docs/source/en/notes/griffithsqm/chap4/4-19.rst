Problem 4.19
==================

For a hydrogen-like atom, the Coulomb potential is

.. math::
    V(r)
    =-\frac{Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0r}.

Thus, we need only replace `e^2` by `Ze^2` in the results for hydrogen. The Bohr energies are

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        E_n(Z)
        &=
        -\frac{m_e}{2\hbar^2}
        \left(\frac{Ze^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\right)^2
        \frac{1}{n^2}
        \\
        &=Z^2E_n(1)
        \\
        &=-\frac{13.6Z^2}{n^2}\ \mathrm{eV}.
    \end{aligned}

In particular, the ground-state energy is

.. math::
    E_1(Z)=-13.6Z^2\ \mathrm{eV},

and the magnitude of the binding energy required to ionize the electron from the ground state is `13.6Z^2\ \mathrm{eV}`. The length corresponding to the Bohr radius is

.. math::
    a(Z)
    =\frac{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar^2}{m_eZe^2}
    =\frac{a}{Z}.

Also, since the wavenumbers of the spectral lines are proportional to the energy differences, the Rydberg constant is

.. math::
    \mathcal{R}(Z)=Z^2\mathcal{R}.

For the Lyman series, `n_f=1`, so the wavelengths scale as

.. math::
    \lambda_Z=\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{H}}}{Z^2}.

The Lyman series of hydrogen extends from the series limit at `91.2\ \mathrm{nm}` to the Lyman `\alpha` line at `121.6\ \mathrm{nm}`. Therefore,

.. math::
    \begin{array}{c|c|c}
        Z&\mathrm{wavelength\ range}&\mathrm{region}
        \\\hline
        2&22.8\text{-}30.4\ \mathrm{nm}
        &\mathrm{extreme\ ultraviolet}
        \\
        3&10.1\text{-}13.5\ \mathrm{nm}
        &\mathrm{extreme\ ultraviolet}
    \end{array}.
