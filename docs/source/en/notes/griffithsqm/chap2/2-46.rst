Problem 2.46
==================

The plane-wave solution is

.. math::
    \psi(x) = Ce^{ikx}.

The periodic boundary condition `\psi(x+L)=\psi(x)` gives

.. math::
    e^{ikL} = 1 \implies k_n = \frac{2\pi n}{L}\quad (n\in\Z).

The normalization condition gives

.. math::
    1 = \int_0^L |C|^2\,\dd x = L|C|^2,

so we may choose

.. math::
    C = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}.

For `n=1,2,\ldots`, define the clockwise and counterclockwise states by

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        \psi_n^+(x) &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}e^{i2\pi nx/L}
        \\
        \psi_n^-(x) &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}e^{-i2\pi nx/L}.
    \end{aligned}

They are orthogonal and linearly independent because

.. math::
    \int_0^L (\psi_n^+)^*\psi_n^-\,\dd x = \frac{1}{L}\int_0^L e^{-i4\pi nx/L}\,\dd x = 0.

Thus, for `n\ge1`, the allowed energy

.. math::
    E_n = \frac{\hbar^2k_n^2}{2m} = \frac{2\pi^2\hbar^2n^2}{mL^2}

is twofold degenerate, while for `n=0`,

.. math::
    \psi_0(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L}},\quad E_0 = 0.

The proof of the theorem in :ref:`notes/griffithsqm/chap2/2-44:Problem 2.44` used the fact that a normalizable bound state on the real line vanishes at infinity. A circle, however, is compact and has no infinity; the wave function is periodic rather than zero. Indeed, the Wronskian of the degenerate plane waves is

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        W &= \psi_n^-\dv{\psi_n^+}{x} - \psi_n^+\dv{\psi_n^-}{x}
        \\
        &= \frac{2ik_n}{L} \neq 0.
    \end{aligned}

Therefore, the theorem in :ref:`notes/griffithsqm/chap2/2-44:Problem 2.44` does not apply to this system, and degeneracy is allowed.
