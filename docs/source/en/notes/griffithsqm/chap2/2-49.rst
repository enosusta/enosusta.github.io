Problem 2.49
==================

a.  For convenience, define

    .. math::
        N \coloneqq \left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4},\quad q(t) \coloneqq x_0e^{-i\omega t}

    and let

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            L &\coloneqq \log \Psi(x,t)
            \\
            &= \log N -\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left[x^2+\frac{x_0^2}{2}+\frac{q^2}{2}+\frac{i\hbar t}{m}-2xq\right]
        \end{aligned}

    Then

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            L_x &= -\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}(x-q)
            \\
            L_{xx} &= -\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}
        \end{aligned}

    Since `\Psi_x=L_x\Psi`,

    .. math::
        \Psi_{xx} = (L_{xx}+L_x^2)\Psi

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \frac{\hat{H}\Psi}{\Psi} &= -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}(L_{xx}+L_x^2)+\frac{1}{2}m\omega^2x^2
            \\
            &= \frac{\hbar\omega}{2}+\frac{m\omega^2}{2}(x-q)^2 +  \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2x^2
            \\
            &= \frac{\hbar\omega}{2}+m\omega^2xq-\frac{1}{2}m\omega^2q^2
        \end{aligned}

    On the other hand, using `q'=-i\omega q`,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            L_t &= -\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left[qq'+\frac{i\hbar}{m}-2xq'\right]
            \\
            &= -\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left[-i\omega q^2+\frac{i\hbar}{m}+2i\omega xq\right]
        \end{aligned}

    Since `\Psi_t=L_t\Psi`,

    .. math::
        \frac{i\hbar\Psi_t}{\Psi} = \frac{\hat{H}\Psi}{\Psi}

    Thus, `\Psi` satisfies the time-dependent Schrödinger equation for the harmonic-oscillator potential.

b.  For the real part of the exponent in `\Psi`, using

    .. math::
        \operatorname{Re}q = x_0\cos(\omega t),\quad \operatorname{Re}q^2 = x_0^2\cos(2\omega t)

    and

    .. math::
        \frac{x_0^2}{2}[1+\cos(2\omega t)] = x_0^2\cos^2(\omega t)

    gives

    .. math::
        x^2+x_0^2\cos^2(\omega t)-2xx_0\cos(\omega t) = (x-x_0\cos(\omega t))^2

    Therefore, the Gaussian probability density is

    .. math::
        |\Psi(x,t)|^2 = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}}\exp\left[-\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}(x-x_0\cos(\omega t))^2\right]

    This wave packet is centered at `x_0\cos(\omega t)` and has variance `\hbar/(2m\omega)`. Thus, it retains its shape while its center follows the motion of a classical harmonic oscillator.

c.  From the result of part (b),

    .. math::
        \braket{x} = x_0\cos(\omega t)

    For the momentum,

    .. math::
        \hat{p}\Psi = -i\hbar \Psi_x = -i\hbar L_x\Psi = im\omega(x-q)\Psi

    and hence

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \braket{p} &= im\omega\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(x-q)|\Psi|^2\,\dd x
            \\
            &= im\omega(\braket{x}-q)
        \end{aligned}

    Since

    .. math::
        q = x_0\cos(\omega t)-ix_0\sin(\omega t)

    it follows that

    .. math::
        \braket{p} = -m\omega x_0\sin(\omega t)

    Taking time derivatives gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \dv{\braket{x}}{t} &= -\omega x_0\sin(\omega t) = \frac{\braket{p}}{m}
            \\
            \dv{\braket{p}}{t} &= -m\omega^2x_0\cos(\omega t) = -m\omega^2\braket{x}
        \end{aligned}

    and therefore

    .. math::
        \dv{\braket{x}}{t} = \frac{\braket{p}}{m},\quad \dv{\braket{p}}{t} = -\braket{\dv{V}{x}}

    so Ehrenfest's theorem is satisfied.
