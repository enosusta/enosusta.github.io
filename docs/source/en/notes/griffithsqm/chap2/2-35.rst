Problem 2.35
==================

a.  The wave function is

    .. math::
        \psi(x) = \begin{cases}
            Ae^{ikx} + Be^{-ikx} & (x<0)
            \\
            Fe^{iqx} & (x>0),
        \end{cases}

    where

    .. math::
        q \coloneqq \frac{\sqrt{2m(E+V_0)}}{\hbar}.

    Continuity of `\psi` and `\psi'` at `x=0` gives

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            A + B &= F
            \\
            k(A-B) &= qF,
        \end{aligned}

    so

    .. math::
        \frac{B}{A} = \frac{k-q}{k+q},\quad \frac{F}{A} = \frac{2k}{k+q}.

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            R = \left|\frac{B}{A}\right|^2 &= \left(\frac{k-q}{k+q}\right)^2
            \\
            &= \left(\frac{\sqrt{E}-\sqrt{E+V_0}}{\sqrt{E}+\sqrt{E+V_0}}\right)^2
            \\
            T = \frac{q}{k}\left|\frac{F}{A}\right|^2 &= \frac{4kq}{(k+q)^2}
            \\
            &= 1-R.
        \end{aligned}

    For `E=V_0/3`,

    .. math::
        \frac{q}{k} = \sqrt{\frac{E+V_0}{E}} = 2,

    so

    .. math::
        R = \left(\frac{1-q/k}{1+q/k}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{9}.

b.  Although `V(x)` changes discontinuously from `0` to `-V_0` the instant the particle passes through `x=0`, the gravitational potential `mgy` changes continuously at a real cliff. Thus, this potential does not correctly represent a cliff.

c.  For a neutron,

    .. math::
        E = 4\,\mathrm{MeV},\quad V_0 = 12\,\mathrm{MeV} = 3E.

    Therefore, as in part (a),

    .. math::
        R = \frac{1}{9}.

    Hence, the probability that the neutron passes through the nuclear surface and is absorbed is

    .. math::
        T = 1-R = \frac{8}{9}.
