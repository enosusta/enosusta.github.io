Problem 2.50
==================

a.  For convenience, define

    .. math::
        y\coloneqq x-vt,\quad \kappa \coloneqq \frac{m\alpha}{\hbar^2}

    and let

    .. math::
        \phi(y) \coloneqq \sqrt{\kappa}e^{-\kappa|y|}

    Then

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\Psi(x,t)|^2\,\dd y &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\phi(y)|^2\,\dd y
            \\
            &= 2\kappa\int_0^{\infty}e^{-2\kappa y}\,\dd y = 1
        \end{aligned}

    so $\Psi(x,t)$ is normalized. From part (b) of :ref:`notes/griffithsqm/chap2/2-23:Problem 2.23`,

    .. math::
        \dv{^2}{y^2}e^{-\kappa|y|} = \kappa^2 e^{-\kappa|y|} - 2\kappa\delta(y)

    and hence

    .. math::
        \phi''(y) = \kappa^2\phi(y) - 2\kappa\phi(0)\delta(y)

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\dv{^2}{y^2}-\alpha\delta(y)\right]\phi(y) &= -\frac{\hbar^2\kappa^2}{2m}\phi(y) + \left(\frac{\hbar^2\kappa}{m}-\alpha\right)\phi(0)\delta(y)
            \\
            &= -\frac{m\alpha^2}{2\hbar^2}\phi(y)
        \end{aligned}

    Next, define the phase factor

    .. math::
        G(x,t) \coloneqq e^{imvx/\hbar}e^{-i(E+mv^2/2)t/\hbar}

    Since `\Psi=G\phi`,

    .. math::
        i\hbar\pdv{\Psi}{t} = G\left[\left(E+\frac{1}{2}mv^2\right)\phi-i\hbar v\phi'\right]

    On the other hand,

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \pdv{\Psi}{x} &= G\left(\phi'+\frac{imv}{\hbar}\phi\right)
            \\
            \pdv{^2\Psi}{x^2} &= G\left(\phi'' + \frac{2imv}{\hbar}\phi' -\frac{m^2v^2}{\hbar^2}\phi\right)
        \end{aligned}

    and therefore

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \hat{H}\Psi &= \left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\pdv{^2}{x^2}-\alpha\delta(x-vt)\right]\Psi
            \\
            &= G\left[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\phi''-\alpha\delta(y)\phi-i\hbar v\phi'+\frac{1}{2}mv^2\phi\right]
            \\
            &= G\left[\left(E+\frac{1}{2}mv^2\right)\phi-i\hbar v\phi'\right]
            \\
            &= i\hbar\pdv{\Psi}{t}
        \end{aligned}

    as required.

b.  Since `\phi(\pm\infty)=0`,

    .. math::
        \braket{p}_\phi = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\phi(-i\hbar\phi')\,\dd y = -\frac{i\hbar}{2}[\phi^2]_{-\infty}^{\infty} = 0

    The phase factor `e^{imvx/\hbar}` shifts the momentum operator as `\hat{p}\\mapsto \hat{p}+mv`, so

    .. math::
        \braket{p^2}_\Psi  = \braket{p^2}_\phi + m^2v^2

    Therefore, the expectation value of the kinetic energy is

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \braket{T}_\Psi &= \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\braket{p^2}_\phi + \frac{1}{2}mv^2
            \\
            &= \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\phi'(y)|^2\,\dd y + \frac{1}{2}mv^2
            \\
            &= \frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\kappa^2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\phi(y)|^2\,\dd y + \frac{1}{2}mv^2
            \\
            &= \frac{m\alpha^2}{2\hbar^2} + \frac{1}{2}mv^2
        \end{aligned}

    The expectation value of the potential energy is

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            \braket{V}_\Psi &= -\alpha\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(x-vt)|\Psi(x,t)|^2\,\dd x
            \\
            &= -\alpha|\phi(0)|^2
            \\
            &= -\alpha\kappa = -\frac{m\alpha^2}{\hbar^2}
        \end{aligned}

    Therefore,

    .. math::
        \braket{H}_\Psi &= \braket{T}_\Psi + \braket{V}_\Psi
        \\
        &= -\frac{m\alpha^2}{2\hbar^2} + \frac{1}{2}mv^2

    Thus, the energy expectation value for the moving delta-function potential is the energy expectation value for the stationary delta-function potential plus the kinetic energy `mv^2/2`.
