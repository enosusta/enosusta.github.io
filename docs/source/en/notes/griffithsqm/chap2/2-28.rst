Problem 2.28
==================

From Equation (2.140), the transmitted- and reflected-wave amplitudes for a single delta-function potential are

.. math::
    t_1 = \frac{1}{1-i\beta},\quad r_1 = \frac{i\beta}{1-i\beta}.

Propagation between the two delta functions introduces the phase factor `e^{2ika}`, so the amplitude for a wave incident from the left to pass through the left delta function and then the right one is

.. math::
    t_1 e^{2ika} t_1 = t_1^2 e^{2ika}.

In general, the amplitude for the wave to make `n` round trips between the delta functions after passing through the left one, and then pass through the right one, is `t_1^2 e^{2ika} (r_1^2 e^{4ika})^n`. Therefore, the amplitude for the incident wave from the left to pass through the right delta function is

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        t &= t_1^2 e^{2ika} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(r_1^2 e^{4ika})^n
        \\
        &= t_1^2 e^{2ika} \frac{1}{1-r_1^2 e^{4ika}}
        \\
        &= \frac{e^{2ika}}{(1-i\beta)^2 + \beta^2 e^{4ika}}.
    \end{aligned}

Let `\theta \coloneqq 2ka`. The denominator then becomes

.. math::
    \begin{aligned}
        (1-i\beta)^2 + \beta^2 e^{2i\theta} &= 1 - 2i\beta - \beta^2 + \beta^2(\cos\theta + i\sin\theta)
        \\
        &= (1-2\beta^2\sin^2\theta) + 2i\beta(-1 + \beta\sin\theta\cos\theta),
    \end{aligned}

so

.. math::
    |(1-i\beta)^2 + \beta^2 e^{4ika}| &= (1-2\beta^2\sin^2\theta)^2 + 4\beta^2(1 - \beta\sin\theta\cos\theta)^2
    \\
    &= 1 + 4\beta^2\cos^2\theta - 8\beta^3\sin\theta\cos\theta + 4\beta^4\sin^2\theta
    \\
    &= 1 + 4\beta^2(\cos\theta-\beta\sin\theta)^2,

and the transmission coefficient is

.. math::
    T = |t|^2 = \frac{1}{1 + 4\beta^2[\cos(2ka)-\beta\sin(2ka)]^2}.

Indeed, as `a\to 0`,

.. math::
    T \to \frac{1}{1 + 4\beta^2},

which agrees with the transmission coefficient for a single delta-function potential of strength `2\alpha`.
