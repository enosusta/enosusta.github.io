Problem 2.53
==================

a.  Since the potential is symmetric,

    .. math::
        S_{11}=S_{22}\eqqcolon r,\quad S_{12}=S_{21}\eqqcolon t.

    and the scattering matrix is

    .. math::
        \bm{S} = \begin{pmatrix}
            r & t
            \\
            t & r
        \end{pmatrix}

    For unit-amplitude incidence from the left on the delta-function potential (2.117), the wave function is

    .. math::
        \psi(x) = \begin{cases}
            e^{ikx} + re^{-ikx} & (x<0)
            \\
            te^{ikx} & (x>0)
        \end{cases}

    The boundary conditions at the origin give

    .. math::
        \begin{aligned}
            1 + r &= t
            \\
            ikt-ik(1-r) &= -\frac{2m\alpha}{\hbar^2}t
        \end{aligned}

    Defining `\beta\coloneqq m\alpha/\hbar^2 k` and substituting the first equation into the second gives

    .. math::
        2ikr = -2ik\beta t \implies r = i\beta t

    and hence

    .. math::
        t = \frac{1}{1-i\beta},\quad r = \frac{i\beta}{1-i\beta}

    Thus,

    .. math::
        \bm{S} = \frac{1}{1-i\beta}\begin{pmatrix}
            i\beta & 1
            \\
            1 & i\beta
        \end{pmatrix}.

b.  For the finite rectangular potential (2.148), define

    .. math::
        k \coloneqq \frac{\sqrt{2mE}}{\hbar},\quad l \coloneqq \frac{\sqrt{2m(E+V_0)}}{\hbar}

    and

    .. math::
        \eta \coloneqq \frac{l^2-k^2}{2kl},\quad \zeta \coloneqq \frac{k^2+l^2}{2kl}

    and let

    .. math::
        D \coloneqq \cos(2la) - i\zeta\sin(2la)

    From :ref:`notes/griffithsqm/chap2/2-32:Problem 2.32`,

    .. math::
        t = \frac{e^{-2ika}}{D},\quad r = i\eta\sin(2la)t

    so

    .. math::
        \bm{S} = \frac{e^{-2ika}}{D}\begin{pmatrix}
            i\eta\sin(2la) & 1
            \\
            1 & i\eta\sin(2la)
        \end{pmatrix}.
